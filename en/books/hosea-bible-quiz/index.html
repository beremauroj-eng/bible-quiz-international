What is Hosea about?

Hosea addresses the northern kingdom of Israel during its final turbulent decades. The prophet’s marriage and children become signs within a larger accusation of covenant unfaithfulness. Courtroom charges, parental imagery, agricultural metaphors and appeals to return expose idolatry, political dependence and injustice while preserving a difficult hope for healing.

A four-part reading map

  1. Hosea 1–3 — family as prophetic sign: Hosea marries Gomer, children receive symbolic names, judgment is reversed in promised reunification and the marriage image returns.
  2. Hosea 4–7 — charges against Israel: lack of knowledge, priestly failure, false worship, unstable politics and superficial repentance.
  3. Hosea 8–11 — consequences and compassion: sowing the wind, Assyrian threat, remembered exodus and the parental portrayal of God teaching Ephraim to walk.
  4. Hosea 12–14 — history, death and return: Jacob traditions, prophetic leadership, final indictment and an invitation to return with words.

Sample questions and answers

Track each metaphor separately—marriage, parent and child, farming, healing and dew—before combining them into a summary of the book.

What was the name of Hosea’s wife?
Answer: Gomer daughter of Diblaim
Reference: Hosea 1:2–3

The marriage introduces the book’s symbolic portrayal of Israel’s covenant unfaithfulness.

What name was given to Hosea’s first son?
Answer: Jezreel
Reference: Hosea 1:4–5

The name points to coming judgment connected with Jehu’s house and the kingdom of Israel.

What two things does God desire instead of sacrifice and burnt offerings?
Answer: Steadfast love or mercy, and knowledge of God
Reference: Hosea 6:4–6

The contrast challenges short-lived loyalty and ritual disconnected from covenant faithfulness.

What does Hosea say Israel reaped after sowing the wind?
Answer: The whirlwind
Reference: Hosea 8:7

The agricultural image introduces failed crops and consequences of Israel’s choices.

What parental action does God recall doing for Ephraim?
Answer: Teaching Ephraim to walk and taking them by the arms
Reference: Hosea 11:1–4

The passage combines parental tenderness with grief that Israel did not recognise its healer.

What are the people told to take with them when returning to the Lord?
Answer: Words
Reference: Hosea 14:1–4

The proposed confession rejects dependence on Assyria, war-horses and manufactured gods.

How to study Hosea

Construct a short timeline of Jeroboam II and the kings named in the opening, then mark references to Assyria and Egypt. Create a metaphor table showing image, human behaviour, divine response and any promised reversal. Pay attention when the text shifts between Israel, Ephraim, Samaria and Judah. Read the symbolic names in chapters 1–2 both in their judgment setting and in the passages that reverse “not my people” and “no mercy.”

Context and interpretation note

Hosea’s marriage commands, family details and sexualised metaphors are difficult and interpreted in different literal, symbolic and literary ways. The prophetic rhetoric reflects an ancient patriarchal setting and must not be used to excuse control, humiliation or violence in modern relationships. Textual uncertainties and abrupt speaker changes also affect translation. The quiz tests named people, images and explicit appeals while separating them from contested reconstructions of Hosea’s private life.
