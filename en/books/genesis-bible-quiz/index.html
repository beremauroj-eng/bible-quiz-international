What is Genesis about?

Origins, covenant promises and the family narratives of Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and Joseph.

This page brings together selected questions about Genesis, with context, references and links for further study. The aim is not to impose a doctrinal interpretation, but to help you recognise what appears in the text and verify each answer in its cited passage.

Questions span different difficulty levels and may cover names, places, narrative sequences, teachings, references and connections within the book itself.

Sample questions and answers

Use these examples as a short diagnostic. Open each question, answer before revealing the panel, and then verify the wording in the cited passage.

Which city was destroyed along with Gomorrah?
Answer: Sodom
Reference: Genesis 19:24–29

The passage names Sodom and Gomorrah together in the account of their destruction.

What new name was given to Jacob?
Answer: Israel
Reference: Genesis 32:28

The name change follows Jacob’s night-long struggle before he meets Esau.

Who killed his brother Abel?
Answer: Cain
Reference: Genesis 4:8

Genesis places the killing in the field after Cain speaks with Abel.

On which day did God rest from the work of creation?
Answer: The seventh day
Reference: Genesis 2:2–3

The seventh day closes the creation sequence and is blessed and made holy in the narrative.

For how many days and nights did rain fall during the flood?
Answer: Forty days and forty nights
Reference: Genesis 7:12

This duration describes the rainfall; the wider flood chronology continues beyond those forty days.

What name did Abraham give to the son Sarah bore him?
Answer: Isaac
Reference: Genesis 21:1–3

Isaac’s birth fulfils the promise repeated in the preceding Abraham narratives.

Whose dreams did Joseph interpret before being placed over Egypt?
Answer: Pharaoh’s dreams
Reference: Genesis 41:15–41

Joseph explains that the two dreams describe one coming sequence: years of abundance followed by famine.

A four-part reading map for Genesis

  1. Genesis 1–11 — beginnings: creation, Eden, Cain and Abel, the flood and Babel.
  2. Genesis 12–25 — Abraham: journeys, covenant promises, family tensions and the birth of Isaac.
  3. Genesis 25–36 — Isaac and Jacob: Esau and Jacob, marriages, children, return to Canaan and reconciliation.
  4. Genesis 37–50 — Joseph: betrayal, Egypt, famine, family reunion and Jacob’s final words.

How to use this quiz as a study tool

Start with a ten-question round before rereading. Record the passages behind missed answers, read those verses in context, and retake the same book without a timer. Separate recognising an answer from being able to explain where it occurs. For narrative-order questions, build a simple sequence of people, journeys and locations instead of memorising isolated names.

Context and careful reading

Genesis moves between primeval narratives and family histories set across the ancient Near East, including Mesopotamia, Canaan and Egypt. Dates and historical reconstructions are discussed differently across traditions, so this quiz concentrates on details that can be checked directly in Genesis. Translation wording can vary; the reference is supplied so learners can compare the wording in the Bible edition they use.
