What is 3 John about?

Third John is a personal letter praising Gaius for walking in truth and supporting travelling coworkers. It exposes Diotrephes, who loves first place, rejects the elder’s authority, spreads malicious charges and excludes hospitable members. Demetrius receives a contrasting good testimony.

This guide treats 3 John as a complete, situated work rather than a bag of isolated sayings. The six questions sample different units and the map locates each answer. Watch repetitions, contrasts, images, connective words and audience shifts. Check the whole paragraph whenever an answer depends on surrounding reasoning, and distinguish visionary description, scriptural quotation, argument and exhortation.

A four-part reading map

  1. 1–4 — greeting and reported faithfulness: beloved Gaius, wellbeing, testimony to truth and joy in faithful children
  2. 5–8 — hospitality as partnership: service to strangers, public witness, sending well and coworkers for truth
  3. 9–10 — Diotrephes and harmful power: desire for primacy, rejection, malicious speech, obstruction and expulsion
  4. 11–15 — imitating good and trusted witness: moral discernment, Demetrius’s testimony and face-to-face plans

Sample questions and answers

Read the cited unit before revealing the answer and explain how it functions within the work.

Whose wellbeing did the elder pray for?
Answer: Gaius’s
Reference: 3 John 1–2

The greeting connects bodily wellbeing with spiritual flourishing.

What gave the elder greatest joy?
Answer: Hearing that his children walked in truth
Reference: 3 John 3–4

Truth is verified in conduct, not merely speech.

Whom had Gaius served faithfully?
Answer: Brothers and sisters, even strangers
Reference: 3 John 5–8

Hospitality made him a coworker with the truth.

Who loved to be first?
Answer: Diotrephes
Reference: 3 John 9–10

His behaviour included rejection, gossip and expulsion.

What should readers imitate?
Answer: What is good, not evil
Reference: 3 John 11

The command introduces the positive witness about Demetrius.

Who had a good testimony from everyone and truth itself?
Answer: Demetrius
Reference: 3 John 12

The elder adds his own trusted testimony.

How to study 3 John

Read 3 John from beginning to end and summarise every mapped section in one sentence. Create columns for people, audience, problem, images, reused Scriptures and community response. Verify all six references in context, record repeated terms and explain how the ending answers the opening. Compare translations when a note changes a key word, but do not build a conclusion on one isolated variant. This process joins recall, literary structure, historical setting, ethics and application without turning a debated reading into an undisputed fact.

Context and interpretation note

The exact offices and relationship among the elder, Gaius and Diotrephes are uncertain. The letter provides a compact case study in abusive leadership: status-seeking, information control, malicious speech, blocked hospitality and punitive exclusion. It should encourage transparent accountability, not allow a new leader to imitate the same coercion.
