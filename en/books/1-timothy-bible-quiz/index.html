What is 1 Timothy about?

First Timothy instructs a delegate confronting speculative teaching in Ephesus. It joins public prayer, household conduct, leadership qualifications, care for widows and elders, warnings about money and a charge to guard what has been entrusted. Character and communal credibility matter throughout.

This guide treats 1 Timothy as a situated argument rather than a collection of slogans. The six questions sample distinct units, while the map locates every answer. Watch repetitions, contrasts, connective words and shifts of audience. Verify an answer across more than one verse whenever its wording depends on the reasoning before or after it.

A four-part reading map

  1. 1 — charge and mercy: false teaching, lawful use of law, Paul’s example and entrusted struggle
  2. 2 — prayer and gathered conduct: intercession for all, peaceful witness and disputed gender instructions
  3. 3 — overseers, deacons and confession: tested character, household care and mystery of godliness
  4. 4–6 — durable care: training, generations, widows, elders, servants, contentment, wealth and deposit

Sample questions and answers

Use each reference as an entry point and read the whole unit before selecting an answer.

What is the goal of the command?
Answer: Love from a pure heart, good conscience and sincere faith
Reference: 1 Timothy 1:3–7

The goal contrasts with speculative controversy.

For whom should prayers be made?
Answer: All people, including kings and authorities
Reference: 1 Timothy 2:1–7

The desired result includes peaceful life and salvation.

What must an overseer be?
Answer: Above reproach, self-controlled and hospitable among other traits
Reference: 1 Timothy 3:1–7

Public and household character outweigh prestige.

What should Timothy train himself for?
Answer: Godliness
Reference: 1 Timothy 4:6–10

Bodily training has value, but godliness bears broader promise.

Which widows should receive formal support?
Answer: Those truly in need under the stated criteria
Reference: 1 Timothy 5:3–16

The passage coordinates family and community responsibility.

What is a root of all kinds of evil?
Answer: The love of money
Reference: 1 Timothy 6:6–10

The warning concerns craving, not money as an object.

How to study 1 Timothy

Read 1 Timothy as a complete letter before isolating memorable lines. Summarise each of the four mapped sections and note who speaks, which problem is answered and what practical consequence follows. Trace connections among charge and mercy, prayer and gathered conduct, overseers, deacons and confession, durable care. For every question, verify the full paragraph, compare transitions and distinguish assertion, quotation, metaphor and exhortation. Record repeated terms and named coworkers, then explain how the closing material returns to concerns introduced near the opening. This method keeps recall, argument, social setting and application in conversation.

Context and interpretation note

Authorship, church order and especially 2:8–15 are interpreted differently. A difficult local instruction must not be used to demean women or conceal abuse. Leadership lists are character tests, not tools for status. Widow criteria arose in a specific welfare setting, while warnings to the rich remain central to responsible application.
