What is Micah about?

Micah speaks to Samaria and Jerusalem during the eighth century BCE. The book exposes land seizure, corrupt leadership, exploitative prophets and confidence unsupported by justice. Judgment speeches alternate with visions of a restored Zion, a ruler connected with Bethlehem, renewed shepherding and a final prayer celebrating forgiveness.

A four-part reading map

  1. Micah 1–2 — coming judgment and seized land: divine appearance, fall of Samaria, lament over Judah’s towns, planned dispossession and a promise of regathering.
  2. Micah 3 — failed leadership: rulers distort justice, prophets speak for payment and Jerusalem’s institutions are condemned.
  3. Micah 4–5 — Zion and future ruler: nations learn peace, exiles gather, conflict gives way to deliverance and Bethlehem becomes central to royal hope.
  4. Micah 6–7 — covenant case and final hope: God’s past faithfulness, required justice, dishonest commerce, social breakdown, waiting and forgiveness.

Sample questions and answers

Notice how Micah pairs judgment and hope; a promise should not be detached from the injustice it answers, and a warning is not the book’s final word.

From what town was the prophet Micah?
Answer: Moresheth
Reference: Micah 1:1

The heading places his ministry during the reigns of Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah.

What happens to the mountains in Micah’s opening vision of the Lord’s coming?
Answer: They melt like wax before fire
Reference: Micah 1:3–4

The valleys split as the imagery portrays creation responding to divine presence.

Which small town is named as the origin of a future ruler?
Answer: Bethlehem Ephrathah
Reference: Micah 5:2–5

The promised ruler will shepherd in strength and is associated with security and peace.

What three-part response does Micah 6:8 say the Lord requires?
Answer: Do justice, love mercy or steadfast kindness, and walk humbly with God
Reference: Micah 6:6–8

The answer contrasts ethical covenant life with increasingly extravagant offerings.

What dishonest business tools are condemned in Micah 6?
Answer: Wicked scales and deceptive weights
Reference: Micah 6:9–12

The accusation connects commercial fraud with violence and lying.

Where does the final prayer say God will cast forgiven sins?
Answer: Into the depths of the sea
Reference: Micah 7:18–20

The closing praise celebrates mercy, compassion and covenant faithfulness.

How to study Micah

Make two columns for judgment and restoration and connect passages that reuse Zion, shepherd, remnant and nations imagery. Map the towns in chapter 1 and note the Hebrew wordplays that translations may explain in footnotes. List the economic actions behind the accusations—coveting fields, seizing houses, bribery and dishonest weights. Compare Micah’s Hezekiah-era setting with 2 Kings and Isaiah, then observe how later writers reuse Bethlehem and peace texts.

Context and interpretation note

Micah’s arrangement, speaker changes and relationship between warning and later hope are interpreted differently. Place-name wordplays and the term translated mercy, kindness or covenant loyalty cannot be reproduced exactly in English. Bethlehem and Zion passages receive distinct Jewish and Christian readings. The quiz tests named locations, accusations and explicit promises in their cited context without reducing Micah 6:8 to a slogan detached from worship, economics and communal responsibility.
