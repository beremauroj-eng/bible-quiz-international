What is 1 John about?

First John addresses a community wounded by separation and competing claims about Jesus and moral life. Its circular meditation connects the witnessed word of life, walking in light, honest confession, Jesus as advocate, obedience, sibling love, testing spirits and grounded assurance without pretending believers never sin.

This guide treats 1 John as a complete, situated work rather than a bag of isolated sayings. The six questions sample different units and the map locates each answer. Watch repetitions, contrasts, images, connective words and audience shifts. Check the whole paragraph whenever an answer depends on surrounding reasoning, and distinguish visionary description, scriptural quotation, argument and exhortation.

A four-part reading map

  1. 1:1–2:2 — witnessed life and light: fellowship, truthful confession, cleansing and advocacy
  2. 2:3–27 — obedience, love and rival claims: new-old command, world’s desires, antichrists and anointing
  3. 2:28–4:6 — children, justice and discernment: hope, sin, sibling love, confidence and testing spirits
  4. 4:7–5:21 — perfected love and assurance: God is love, testimony, overcoming faith, prayer and true God

Sample questions and answers

Read the cited unit before revealing the answer and explain how it functions within the work.

What was proclaimed from eyewitness experience?
Answer: The word of life
Reference: 1 John 1:1–4

Hearing, seeing and touching ground fellowship and joy.

What happens when sins are confessed?
Answer: God forgives and cleanses
Reference: 1 John 1:8–10

Confession rejects the false claim of sinlessness.

Who is the advocate with the Father?
Answer: Jesus Christ the righteous
Reference: 1 John 2:1–2

The assurance aims to prevent sin without producing despair.

How is passage from death to life known?
Answer: By love for brothers and sisters
Reference: 1 John 3:11–18

Love becomes concrete through costly help.

How should spirits be tested?
Answer: By their confession about Jesus Christ come in the flesh
Reference: 1 John 4:1–6

Not every spiritual claim is trustworthy.

What casts out fear?
Answer: Perfect love
Reference: 1 John 4:16–18

The paragraph relates fear to punishment and maturing love.

How to study 1 John

Read 1 John from beginning to end and summarise every mapped section in one sentence. Create columns for people, audience, problem, images, reused Scriptures and community response. Verify all six references in context, record repeated terms and explain how the ending answers the opening. Compare translations when a note changes a key word, but do not build a conclusion on one isolated variant. This process joins recall, literary structure, historical setting, ethics and application without turning a debated reading into an undisputed fact.

Context and interpretation note

The work’s exact genre, opponents and references to sin “leading to death” are debated. Labels such as antichrist describe a specific rupture over confession and must not become slurs for political or religious enemies. Assurance should coexist with truthful confession; protecting communal identity never excuses hatred, fear or concealment of harm.
