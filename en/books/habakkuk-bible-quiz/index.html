What is Habakkuk about?

Habakkuk presents a dialogue in which the prophet challenges God about violence and failed justice, then struggles with the announcement that the Babylonians will act as instruments of judgment. A watchful wait receives a vision contrasting arrogant power with faithful endurance. Five woes condemn exploitation before a closing prayer transforms remembered fear into resilient trust.

A three-part reading map

  1. Habakkuk 1 — two complaints and an answer: the prophet asks how long violence will continue, Babylon is announced, and the cure appears more troubling than the disease.
  2. Habakkuk 2 — watch, vision and woes: the message is written clearly, the righteous live by faithfulness, and plunder, greed, bloodshed, humiliation and idolatry are condemned.
  3. Habakkuk 3 — prayer and trust: a hymn recalls divine action, creation trembles, the prophet waits through fear and resolves to rejoice despite agricultural collapse.

Sample questions and answers

Follow the questions as questions; Habakkuk models honest protest that waits for an answer rather than pretending violence and injustice are easy to explain.

What repeated question opens Habakkuk’s complaint?
Answer: “How long?”
Reference: Habakkuk 1:1–4

The prophet cries about violence, wrongdoing, strife and justice that comes out distorted.

Which people does God say are being raised up in the first answer?
Answer: The Chaldeans or Babylonians
Reference: Habakkuk 1:5–11

They are described as swift and fearsome, seizing dwellings and attributing strength to their god.

Where does Habakkuk position himself while waiting for the next answer?
Answer: At his watchpost or on the watchtower
Reference: Habakkuk 2:1

He waits to see how God will respond to his complaint.

What is Habakkuk told to do with the vision?
Answer: Write it clearly on tablets so a reader can run with it
Reference: Habakkuk 2:2–3

The vision has an appointed time and is to be awaited even if it seems delayed.

How does Habakkuk contrast the righteous person with the proud?
Answer: The righteous will live by faith or faithfulness
Reference: Habakkuk 2:4

The proud person is described as internally crooked, unlike the enduring righteous.

What does Habakkuk resolve to do even if fields and flocks fail?
Answer: Rejoice in the Lord and take joy in God his salvation
Reference: Habakkuk 3:17–19

The closing confidence does not deny loss but declares strength within it.

How to study Habakkuk

Arrange the book as complaint, answer, complaint, waiting, vision and prayer. List the forms of violence in chapter 1 and compare them with the five woes in chapter 2; the empire used to judge injustice is itself held accountable. Mark quotations or echoes in Romans, Galatians and Hebrews only after examining the Hebrew phrase in its setting. In chapter 3, identify memories of divine action and the movement from trembling to trust.

Context and interpretation note

The phrase translated “live by faith,” “faithfulness” or “his faith” has a significant interpretive history, and later biblical writers apply it in new arguments. The identity, timing and role of Babylon are historically situated, not a label for any disliked modern nation. The final theophany uses ancient poetic imagery. Quiz answers stay with explicit speakers and statements while recognising that lament, divine agency and the relationship between faith and faithfulness require careful interpretation.
