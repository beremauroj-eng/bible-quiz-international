What is Ezra about?

Ezra narrates two major returns to Jerusalem under Persian rule. The first group rebuilds the altar and temple despite local opposition; decades later Ezra arrives with authority to teach and administer the law. Lists, official letters, public worship and a difficult community crisis all contribute to the account.

A four-part reading map

  1. Ezra 1–2 — decree and return: Cyrus authorises the temple project, sacred vessels are restored and returning families are counted.
  2. Ezra 3–6 — altar and temple: worship resumes, foundations are laid, opposition interrupts construction and Darius confirms the earlier decree.
  3. Ezra 7–8 — Ezra’s journey: the scribe receives royal authority, gathers companions, proclaims a fast and brings gifts safely to Jerusalem.
  4. Ezra 9–10 — confession and response: Ezra learns of intermarriage, prays publicly and the assembly addresses the covenant crisis.

Sample questions and answers

Follow the two return movements separately so that Zerubbabel’s temple work and Ezra’s later teaching mission do not collapse into one event.

Which Persian king authorised the first return and rebuilding of the temple?
Answer: Cyrus king of Persia
Reference: Ezra 1:1–4

Cyrus issued a proclamation permitting people to go to Jerusalem and support the project.

What did the returned community rebuild before laying the temple foundation?
Answer: The altar of the God of Israel
Reference: Ezra 3:1–6

They offered burnt offerings and observed appointed festivals while the temple was not yet founded.

How did the people respond when the temple foundation was laid?
Answer: Many shouted for joy, while older people who remembered the former temple wept
Reference: Ezra 3:10–13

The sounds of joy and weeping were so loud that they could not easily be distinguished.

Which two prophets encouraged the renewed temple construction?
Answer: Haggai and Zechariah
Reference: Ezra 5:1–2

Zerubbabel and Jeshua resumed building with the prophets supporting them.

What special skill does the book attribute to Ezra?
Answer: He was a scribe skilled in the law of Moses
Reference: Ezra 7:1–10

Ezra had devoted himself to studying, practising and teaching the law.

Why did Ezra proclaim a fast beside the Ahava canal?
Answer: To humble the travellers and ask God for a safe journey
Reference: Ezra 8:21–23

The group sought protection for themselves, their children and their possessions before departing.

How to study Ezra

Build a timeline with two columns: the return associated with Zerubbabel and the later arrival of Ezra. Place Persian rulers, prophetic activity and construction stages beside that timeline. Read the Aramaic correspondence in chapters 4–6 as official documents embedded in the narrative. For each list, identify its purpose—community identity, accountability, temple service or custody of valuable objects—rather than memorising names without context.

Context and interpretation note

Ezra’s final chapters concern marriage, ancestry and covenant boundaries in a post-exilic community. Their language and actions raise historical, ethical and pastoral questions that interpreters address differently, and they must not be repurposed to legitimise modern racial prejudice. Names, totals and the sequence of Persian correspondence also present textual and chronological complexities. The quiz confines factual answers to what cited passages state and signals disputed conclusions rather than disguising them as certainty.
