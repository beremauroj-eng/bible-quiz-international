What is Acts about?

Acts continues Luke’s account from Jesus’ ascension to Paul’s witness in Rome. The Holy Spirit empowers a diverse movement that begins in Jerusalem, crosses social and geographic boundaries and repeatedly negotiates what Gentile inclusion means. Speeches, journeys, conflicts, imprisonments and legal hearings show the message advancing despite opposition.

A four-part reading map

  1. Acts 1–7 — witness in Jerusalem: ascension, Pentecost, shared life, temple proclamation, internal crises, seven appointed servants and Stephen’s death.
  2. Acts 8–12 — Judea, Samaria and new crossings: Philip, Ethiopian official, Saul’s call, Peter and Cornelius, Antioch and Peter’s escape.
  3. Acts 13–20 — commissioned journeys: Barnabas and Paul, synagogue and Gentile audiences, Jerusalem council, Macedonia, Athens, Corinth and Ephesus.
  4. Acts 21–28 — arrest and journey to Rome: Jerusalem conflict, hearings before governors and king, dangerous voyage, Malta and guarded ministry in Rome.

Sample questions and answers

Track repeated summaries of growth and distinguish descriptive events from commands; Acts narrates development across different settings rather than prescribing one identical sequence for every community.

What geographic pattern did Jesus give for the disciples’ witness?
Answer: Jerusalem, Judea and Samaria, and the ends of the earth
Reference: Acts 1:6–9

The promise connects the Holy Spirit’s power with the movement of the narrative.

What visible and audible signs accompanied Pentecost?
Answer: A sound like rushing wind, tongues like fire and speech in other languages
Reference: Acts 2:1–13

Visitors from many regions heard the disciples declaring God’s works in their own languages.

Who was killed after a speech reviewing Israel’s history?
Answer: Stephen
Reference: Acts 6:8–7:60

Witnesses placed their garments near Saul, who approved the killing.

Whom did Philip meet reading Isaiah on the road from Jerusalem to Gaza?
Answer: An Ethiopian court official or eunuch
Reference: Acts 8:26–40

Philip explained the passage, and the traveller requested baptism.

Which Roman centurion received Peter after parallel visions?
Answer: Cornelius
Reference: Acts 10:1–48

The Spirit fell on the gathered Gentiles, reshaping the community’s understanding of inclusion.

What central issue did the Jerusalem council address?
Answer: Whether Gentile believers had to be circumcised and keep the law of Moses
Reference: Acts 15:1–35

The apostles and elders sent a letter with a limited set of requirements and encouragement.

How to study Acts

Map every city and draw arrows for Philip, Peter, Barnabas and Paul. Keep a speech table listing audience, scriptural argument, response and narrative outcome. Mark Spirit guidance, prayer, meals, opposition and legal protection. Compare Paul’s conversion accounts in chapters 9, 22 and 26 as speeches tailored to different audiences rather than treating variations as accidental duplication. Read the open-ended Rome conclusion as part of Luke’s literary purpose.

Context and interpretation note

Acts selects episodes and speeches rather than giving a complete institutional history. Chronology, historical detail, communal property, Spirit reception and the council’s requirements are interpreted differently. Conflict language involving “the Jews” refers to specific first-century disputes and must not support antisemitism. The quiz tests Acts’ own sequence and explanations, distinguishing narrative description from universal command and avoiding harmonisation that erases different speeches.
