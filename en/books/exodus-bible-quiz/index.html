What is Exodus about?

Exodus follows Israel from forced labour in Egypt through deliverance, covenant at Sinai and the construction of the tabernacle. Moses’ call, the confrontation with Pharaoh, Passover, the sea crossing and the wilderness journey form one connected narrative rather than isolated stories.

A four-part reading map

  1. Exodus 1–6 — oppression and calling: Israel’s labour, Moses’ early life, the burning bush and the first confrontation with Pharaoh.
  2. Exodus 7–15 — plagues and departure: signs in Egypt, Passover, the exodus and the song after crossing the sea.
  3. Exodus 16–24 — wilderness and covenant: manna, water, Sinai, the Ten Commandments and covenant instructions.
  4. Exodus 25–40 — tabernacle and presence: design instructions, the golden calf, covenant renewal, construction and completion.

Sample questions and answers

Answer first, then open the panel and read enough of the cited passage to see where the detail fits in the wider episode.

Who found the infant Moses among the reeds by the Nile?
Answer: Pharaoh’s daughter
Reference: Exodus 2:5–10

She arranged for Moses to be nursed and later brought him into Pharaoh’s household.

At what mountain did Moses encounter the burning bush?
Answer: Horeb, called the mountain of God
Reference: Exodus 3:1–4

Moses was tending Jethro’s flock when he came to Horeb and saw the bush burning without being consumed.

What happened to the water of the Nile in the first plague?
Answer: It was turned to blood
Reference: Exodus 7:14–24

The episode begins the sequence of plagues that confronts Pharaoh’s refusal to release Israel.

Where were the Israelites instructed to put the Passover lamb’s blood?
Answer: On the two doorposts and the lintel
Reference: Exodus 12:7, 12–13

The blood is identified as a sign on the houses during the Passover instructions.

How did the Lord lead Israel by day and by night?
Answer: By a pillar of cloud by day and a pillar of fire by night
Reference: Exodus 13:21–22

The two signs guided the people and did not depart from before them.

What was different about the manna gathered on the sixth day?
Answer: The people gathered twice as much
Reference: Exodus 16:22–26

The double portion prepared for the seventh day, when manna was not to be gathered.

How to study Exodus

Track repeated negotiations between Moses and Pharaoh, noting what is commanded, refused and then repeated. Build a journey line from Egypt to Sinai, and keep the covenant material connected to the deliverance narrative that precedes it. For the tabernacle chapters, compare the instructions in chapters 25–31 with their fulfilment in chapters 35–40.

Translation and context note

Bible translations may use “Red Sea” or “Sea of Reeds” for the body of water in the crossing narrative, and they may render some tabernacle terms differently. Questions therefore cite the chapter and avoid treating one English wording as the only valid form. Chronological and geographical reconstructions are debated, so this page distinguishes direct textual details from later proposals.
