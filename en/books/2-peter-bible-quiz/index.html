What is 2 Peter about?

Second Peter presents a final apostolic reminder calling believers to grow in virtue and knowledge. It appeals to the transfiguration and prophetic Scripture, denounces exploitative teachers with judgment traditions, answers scoffing about delayed fulfilment and directs hope toward new heavens and a new earth marked by justice.

This guide treats 2 Peter as a complete, situated work rather than a bag of isolated sayings. The six questions sample different units and the map locates each answer. Watch repetitions, contrasts, images, connective words and audience shifts. Check the whole paragraph whenever an answer depends on surrounding reasoning, and distinguish visionary description, scriptural quotation, argument and exhortation.

A four-part reading map

  1. 1 — calling and reliable witness: divine provision, chain of virtues, remembrance, transfiguration and prophecy
  2. 2 — destructive teachers: secret denial, greed, earlier judgments, Balaam, empty promises and corruption
  3. 3:1–10 — remembered promise: prophets and apostles, scoffers, creation, divine patience and the day
  4. 3:11–18 — holy expectation: transformed creation, justice, difficult Pauline letters and growth in grace

Sample questions and answers

Read the cited unit before revealing the answer and explain how it functions within the work.

What should be added to faith first in the virtue chain?
Answer: Goodness or virtue
Reference: 2 Peter 1:3–8

Knowledge, self-control, endurance, godliness, affection and love follow.

What event did the author claim to witness?
Answer: Jesus receiving honour on the holy mountain
Reference: 2 Peter 1:16–18

The description recalls the transfiguration.

How did prophecy come?
Answer: People spoke from God as carried by the Holy Spirit
Reference: 2 Peter 1:19–21

The claim rejects prophecy originating merely in human will.

Which prophet’s donkey appears in the warning?
Answer: Balaam’s
Reference: 2 Peter 2:15–16

The example exposes profit-seeking madness.

Why is the Lord described as patient?
Answer: Not wanting any to perish but all to repent
Reference: 2 Peter 3:8–10

Divine time answers the charge of delay.

What should believers look for?
Answer: New heavens and a new earth where righteousness dwells
Reference: 2 Peter 3:11–13

Hope motivates holy and godly lives.

How to study 2 Peter

Read 2 Peter from beginning to end and summarise every mapped section in one sentence. Create columns for people, audience, problem, images, reused Scriptures and community response. Verify all six references in context, record repeated terms and explain how the ending answers the opening. Compare translations when a note changes a key word, but do not build a conclusion on one isolated variant. This process joins recall, literary structure, historical setting, ethics and application without turning a debated reading into an undisputed fact.

Context and interpretation note

Authorship and literary dependence on Jude are debated. Prophecy language does not remove the need for careful interpretation. Judgment rhetoric must not authorise violence against alleged false teachers, and apocalyptic fire should not encourage environmental neglect. The letter links expectation with justice, patience and moral growth rather than date-setting.
