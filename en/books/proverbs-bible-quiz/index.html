What is Proverbs about?

Proverbs gathers instruction and concise sayings about forming wise character in everyday life. Its subjects include speech, work, money, friendship, family, sexuality, justice, leadership and disciplined learning. The book presents wisdom as skill shaped by reverence for God, careful observation, correction and choices repeated over time.

A five-part reading map

  1. Proverbs 1–9 — extended instruction: parental appeals, competing invitations from Wisdom and Folly, warnings about violence, adultery and careless choices.
  2. Proverbs 10:1–22:16 — sayings of Solomon: short comparisons covering words, diligence, wealth, integrity, relationships and rulers.
  3. Proverbs 22:17–24:34 — sayings of the wise: longer counsels, boundary warnings, table manners, discipline and observations about laziness.
  4. Proverbs 25–29 — Hezekiah’s collection: Solomonic sayings copied by royal officials, with attention to conflict, leadership and self-control.
  5. Proverbs 30–31 — Agur and Lemuel: numerical sayings, royal instruction and an alphabetic poem celebrating a capable, God-fearing woman.

Sample questions and answers

Ask whether a saying presents a contrast, comparison, observation or command; neighbouring proverbs can deliberately place different situations side by side.

What does Proverbs call the beginning of knowledge?
Answer: The fear of the Lord
Reference: Proverbs 1:7

The verse contrasts this foundation with fools despising wisdom and instruction.

Which small creature is the sluggard told to observe?
Answer: The ant
Reference: Proverbs 6:6–11

The ant’s preparation illustrates diligence without an overseer compelling the work.

What kind of answer turns away wrath?
Answer: A gentle or soft answer
Reference: Proverbs 15:1

The contrasting line warns that a harsh word stirs up anger.

What does Proverbs compare to one person sharpening another?
Answer: Iron sharpening iron
Reference: Proverbs 27:17

The image describes the effect people can have on one another.

Whose sayings were copied by the officials of King Hezekiah?
Answer: Solomon’s
Reference: Proverbs 25:1

The heading introduces the collection extending through chapter 29.

What quality is praised above deceptive charm and fading beauty in the final poem?
Answer: A woman who fears the Lord
Reference: Proverbs 31:30–31

The conclusion calls for her work and character to receive public recognition.

How to study Proverbs

Create topic chains for speech, wealth, friendship, anger, justice and leadership instead of reading every isolated line as unrelated. Note parallelism: the second line may repeat, contrast or complete the first. Compare sayings that seem to pull in different directions, such as the paired instructions about answering a fool in Proverbs 26, because wisdom includes judging the situation. Convert one proverb into a week-long observation exercise and record where its pattern holds or where other biblical considerations add complexity.

Context and interpretation note

A proverb usually expresses a reliable pattern, not an unconditional promise that eliminates circumstance, injustice or human freedom. Ancient household, royal and economic settings also differ from modern life, and some images or discipline language require ethical care. Hebrew wordplay and compact parallel lines can produce legitimate translation differences. Quiz questions test wording, speakers, headings and stated comparisons without turning every observation into a guarantee or using isolated sayings to blame people for suffering.
