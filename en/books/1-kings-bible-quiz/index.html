What is 1 Kings about?

1 Kings begins with Solomon’s accession, wisdom, building projects and temple dedication. After his death the kingdom divides, and the narrative alternates between rulers of Israel and Judah before giving extended attention to Elijah’s conflict with Ahab’s household.

A four-part reading map

  1. 1 Kings 1–4 — Solomon established: succession, rival claims, wisdom and administration.
  2. 1 Kings 5–11 — temple and turning: construction, dedication, royal splendour, the Queen of Sheba and Solomon’s later unfaithfulness.
  3. 1 Kings 12–16 — divided kingdoms: Rehoboam and Jeroboam, rival worship centres and successive rulers.
  4. 1 Kings 17–22 — Elijah and Ahab: drought, Carmel, Horeb, Naboth’s vineyard and the final battle.

Sample questions and answers

Place each answer within the transition from one kingdom under Solomon to two kingdoms and prophetic confrontation.

What did Solomon request from God at Gibeon?
Answer: An understanding or discerning heart to govern
Reference: 1 Kings 3:4–15

Solomon asked for discernment rather than long life, riches or the death of enemies.

What major building did Solomon complete in Jerusalem?
Answer: The temple of the Lord
Reference: 1 Kings 6:1–38

The chapter describes the structure and states that it took seven years to build.

Who visited Solomon with difficult questions after hearing of his fame?
Answer: The Queen of Sheba
Reference: 1 Kings 10:1–10

She tested Solomon, observed his wisdom and household, and exchanged gifts.

Who became king of the northern tribes after the kingdom divided?
Answer: Jeroboam son of Nebat
Reference: 1 Kings 12:16–20

Rehoboam retained Judah while the northern tribes made Jeroboam king.

On which mountain did Elijah confront the prophets of Baal?
Answer: Mount Carmel
Reference: 1 Kings 18:19–39

The contest concerned which deity would answer by fire.

Whose vineyard did Ahab desire next to his palace?
Answer: Naboth’s vineyard
Reference: 1 Kings 21:1–16

Naboth refused to surrender his ancestral inheritance, after which Jezebel arranged false accusations.

How to study 1 Kings

Maintain two columns after chapter 12—Israel in the north and Judah in the south—and record each ruler, capital, prophet and major conflict. Before the division, trace how Solomon’s wisdom and temple achievements sit alongside warnings and later choices. For Elijah, connect each location and royal encounter rather than memorising miracles separately.

Translation and chronology note

Royal names, regnal formulas and chronological totals require careful comparison; scholars propose different solutions for overlapping reigns and calendar systems. Chronicles also presents some events from a different perspective. Questions focus on the sequence and statements of 1 Kings and avoid presenting one reconstructed chronology as indisputable.
