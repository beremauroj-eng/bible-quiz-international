What is Leviticus about?

Leviticus presents instructions for worship and community life centred on the tabernacle. Offerings, priestly service, ritual purity, the Day of Atonement, ethical commands, festivals and land practices are arranged around the call for Israel to be holy.

A six-part reading map

  1. Leviticus 1–7: offerings and the responsibilities of worshippers and priests.
  2. Leviticus 8–10: ordination of Aaron’s household and the deaths of Nadab and Abihu.
  3. Leviticus 11–15: food, childbirth, skin conditions and bodily discharges.
  4. Leviticus 16: the annual Day of Atonement.
  5. Leviticus 17–22: blood, relationships, justice and priestly holiness.
  6. Leviticus 23–27: appointed times, lamps and bread, land years, vows and dedications.

Sample questions and answers

These questions deliberately cover different sections so the quiz tests the shape of the whole book.

Which family was ordained to serve as priests?
Answer: Aaron and his sons
Reference: Leviticus 8:1–13

The ordination includes washing, garments, anointing and the clothing of Aaron’s sons.

What did Nadab and Abihu offer before the Lord?
Answer: Unauthorised fire
Reference: Leviticus 10:1–2

The text states that the fire had not been commanded, and both men died before the Lord.

Which chapter centres on the Day of Atonement?
Answer: Leviticus 16
Reference: Leviticus 16:1–34

The chapter gives instructions for Aaron, the sanctuary, the sacrifices and the annual observance.

According to Leviticus 19:18, whom should a person love as oneself?
Answer: One’s neighbour
Reference: Leviticus 19:18

The command appears after prohibitions against vengeance and bearing a grudge.

Which chapter lists the appointed festivals, including Passover, Weeks and Booths?
Answer: Leviticus 23
Reference: Leviticus 23:1–44

The chapter gathers Sabbath and annual appointed times into one sequence.

What was the land to receive every seventh year?
Answer: A Sabbath of complete rest
Reference: Leviticus 25:1–7

The instruction applies Sabbath rhythm to the land after Israel enters the land.

How to study Leviticus

Create separate columns for people, places, actions and time. Ask who performs each ritual, where it occurs, what is brought or done, and whether it is daily, annual or linked to a particular condition. Then compare legal sections with nearby narrative episodes. This prevents rules from becoming an unstructured list and makes repeated patterns easier to retrieve.

Translation and interpretation note

Technical terms for offerings, purity conditions and sanctuary spaces vary among translations. “Clean” and “unclean” in these chapters describe ritual categories and should not automatically be treated as synonyms for moral innocence and guilt. Questions stay close to the cited wording and avoid presenting debated modern applications as if they were explicit quiz answers.
