What is John about?

John presents selected signs and extended conversations so readers may believe that Jesus is the Messiah and Son of God and find life in his name. A poetic prologue introduces the Word, light and witness. Public signs and disputes lead into private farewell teaching, crucifixion, resurrection appearances and a concluding restoration by the sea.

A four-part reading map

  1. John 1 — prologue and first witnesses: the Word becomes flesh, John testifies and the first disciples follow.
  2. John 2–12 — signs before the world: Cana, temple, new birth, living water, bread, festival disputes, healed sight, Lazarus and entry into Jerusalem.
  3. John 13–17 — farewell: foot washing, new commandment, promised Advocate, vine imagery and Jesus’ prayer for disciples.
  4. John 18–21 — passion and resurrection: arrest, Roman trial, crucifixion, burial, empty tomb, appearances, Thomas and breakfast by the sea.

Sample questions and answers

Track each sign, the response it produces and the discourse that interprets it; John’s symbols often work on more than one level.

What does the prologue say the Word became?
Answer: Flesh, dwelling among us
Reference: John 1:1–18

The prologue identifies the Word with God, creation, life and light before describing incarnation.

What was Jesus’ first sign in John?
Answer: Turning water into wine at Cana
Reference: John 2:1–11

The sign revealed glory and the disciples believed in him.

What did Jesus tell Nicodemus was necessary to see God’s kingdom?
Answer: Being born from above or born again
Reference: John 3:1–10

The Greek expression can carry both meanings, contributing to Nicodemus’s misunderstanding.

Whom did Jesus call out of the tomb after four days?
Answer: Lazarus
Reference: John 11:1–44

Before the sign Jesus tells Martha that he is the resurrection and the life.

What servant action did Jesus perform during the final meal?
Answer: Washing the disciples’ feet
Reference: John 13:1–17

Jesus presents the action as an example of mutual service.

What confession did Thomas make when he saw the risen Jesus?
Answer: “My Lord and my God”
Reference: John 20:24–31

The scene leads into the Gospel’s purpose statement about believing and life.

How to study John

Create a sign chart with action, location, witnesses, response and interpreting conversation. Track paired themes—light and darkness, above and below, belief and unbelief, life and death—and note misunderstandings that allow deeper explanation. List every “I am” saying with its immediate setting. Compare the passion timeline and temple action with the Synoptics without forcing identical chronology before hearing John’s literary purpose.

Context and interpretation note

John’s language about “the Jews” usually concerns specific first-century leaders or groups within an intra-Jewish conflict and must never authorise antisemitism. Authorship, chronology, sacramental readings and the relationship between signs and history are debated. The woman caught in adultery in John 7:53–8:11 is absent from the earliest manuscripts and should be labelled accordingly. The quiz follows the cited textual scope and distinguishes John’s account from harmonised tradition.
