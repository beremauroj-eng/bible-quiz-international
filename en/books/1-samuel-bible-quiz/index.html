What is 1 Samuel about?

1 Samuel moves from the period of the judges to Israel’s monarchy. Samuel’s birth and prophetic ministry, the ark narratives, the people’s request for a king, Saul’s rise and decline, and David’s emergence form a sustained account of changing leadership.

A three-part reading map

  1. 1 Samuel 1–7 — Samuel and the ark: Hannah, Samuel’s call, Eli’s household, capture and return of the ark, and victory at Mizpah.
  2. 1 Samuel 8–15 — Saul becomes king: the request for monarchy, Saul’s anointing, early victories and Samuel’s confrontations with him.
  3. 1 Samuel 16–31 — David and Saul: David’s anointing, Goliath, Jonathan’s friendship, Saul’s pursuit and the final battle.

Sample questions and answers

Use the references to keep Samuel, Saul and David within the correct phase of the leadership transition.

Who prayed for a son and later dedicated Samuel to the Lord’s service?
Answer: Hannah
Reference: 1 Samuel 1:9–28

Hannah prayed at Shiloh, named her son Samuel and later brought him to serve there.

Whose voice did Samuel initially think he heard during the night?
Answer: Eli’s voice
Reference: 1 Samuel 3:1–10

Samuel went to Eli three times before Eli understood that the Lord was calling the boy.

Which people captured the ark after defeating Israel?
Answer: The Philistines
Reference: 1 Samuel 4:1–11

The ark was taken and Eli’s sons Hophni and Phinehas died in the battle.

Who anointed Saul as Israel’s first king?
Answer: Samuel
Reference: 1 Samuel 9:15–10:1

Samuel privately anointed Saul before Saul was publicly selected before the tribes.

What weapon did David use to strike Goliath?
Answer: A sling and a stone
Reference: 1 Samuel 17:40–50

David selected stones from the stream and struck Goliath in the forehead.

What did David cut from Saul while sparing him in the cave?
Answer: The corner of Saul’s robe
Reference: 1 Samuel 24:1–12

David later showed the piece to demonstrate that he had refused to kill Saul.

How to study 1 Samuel

Create parallel timelines for Samuel, Saul and David. Note each anointing, public confirmation, act of obedience or refusal, and change in relationship. Pay special attention to speeches and songs: Hannah’s prayer, Samuel’s warnings, Saul’s explanations and David’s appeals reveal how the narrative evaluates leadership.

Translation and interpretation note

English editions may number a few verses differently and vary in names or military terminology. Ancient manuscript traditions also preserve notable differences in parts of the David and Goliath narrative. Questions focus on details clearly represented in commonly used editions and avoid reducing the book’s complex portrayal of kingship to slogans.
