What is 1 Peter about?

First Peter addresses communities in Asia Minor as chosen exiles. It grounds living hope in resurrection, reworks scriptural identity for a diverse people, urges honourable public conduct, speaks into unequal households, interprets unjust suffering through Christ and calls elders and all believers to humble mutual care.

This guide treats 1 Peter as a complete, situated work rather than a bag of isolated sayings. The six questions sample different units and the map locates each answer. Watch repetitions, contrasts, images, connective words and audience shifts. Check the whole paragraph whenever an answer depends on surrounding reasoning, and distinguish visionary description, scriptural quotation, argument and exhortation.

A four-part reading map

  1. 1 — living hope and holy conduct: new birth, guarded inheritance, tested faith, prophets and enduring word
  2. 2 — people and public witness: living stones, royal priesthood, honourable life and household vulnerability
  3. 3 — relationships under pressure: spouses, shared virtues, defence of hope, suffering and baptism imagery
  4. 4–5 — resilient community: hospitality, gifts, fiery trial, elders, humility, vigilance and restoration

Sample questions and answers

Read the cited unit before revealing the answer and explain how it functions within the work.

Into what has God given new birth?
Answer: A living hope through Jesus Christ’s resurrection
Reference: 1 Peter 1:3–9

The hope includes an imperishable inheritance.

What are believers like in the spiritual house?
Answer: Living stones
Reference: 1 Peter 2:4–10

Temple and priesthood images describe shared vocation.

What should believers be ready to explain?
Answer: The reason for their hope
Reference: 1 Peter 3:13–17

The answer should be given gently and respectfully.

What does baptism represent as saving?
Answer: An appeal or pledge of a good conscience through resurrection
Reference: 1 Peter 3:18–22

The text explicitly denies that mere removal of dirt is the point.

How should gifts be used?
Answer: To serve one another as stewards of grace
Reference: 1 Peter 4:7–11

Speaking and serving should direct glory to God.

How should elders lead?
Answer: Willingly, eagerly and by example, not domination
Reference: 1 Peter 5:1–4

The chief shepherd image limits coercive leadership.

How to study 1 Peter

Read 1 Peter from beginning to end and summarise every mapped section in one sentence. Create columns for people, audience, problem, images, reused Scriptures and community response. Verify all six references in context, record repeated terms and explain how the ending answers the opening. Compare translations when a note changes a key word, but do not build a conclusion on one isolated variant. This process joins recall, literary structure, historical setting, ethics and application without turning a debated reading into an undisputed fact.

Context and interpretation note

Authorship, social setting, “spirits in prison” and baptism language are debated. Household and slavery passages address people within coercive systems and must never command victims to remain in danger or validate abuse. Suffering for doing good is not the same as preventable harm imposed by leaders; elders are explicitly forbidden to dominate.
