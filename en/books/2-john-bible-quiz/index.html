What is 2 John about?

Second John is a compact letter to the chosen lady and her children, possibly a congregation personified. It joins truth and love, defines love as walking according to God’s commands, warns about teachers denying Jesus Christ’s coming in flesh and sets boundaries around participation in destructive teaching.

This guide treats 2 John as a complete, situated work rather than a bag of isolated sayings. The six questions sample different units and the map locates each answer. Watch repetitions, contrasts, images, connective words and audience shifts. Check the whole paragraph whenever an answer depends on surrounding reasoning, and distinguish visionary description, scriptural quotation, argument and exhortation.

A four-part reading map

  1. 1–3 — greeting in truth and love: the elder, chosen lady, children, shared truth, grace, mercy and peace
  2. 4–6 — walking in the command: joy, no novel command, mutual love and obedient practice
  3. 7–11 — deceivers and boundaries: incarnation confession, guarding work, remaining in teaching and hospitality
  4. 12–13 — embodied fellowship: limits of paper and ink, face-to-face joy and greetings from a sister community

Sample questions and answers

Read the cited unit before revealing the answer and explain how it functions within the work.

Who writes the letter?
Answer: The elder
Reference: 2 John 1–3

The recipients are the chosen lady and her children.

What brought the elder joy?
Answer: Finding some children walking in truth
Reference: 2 John 4

Their conduct matched the command received from the Father.

What command was not new?
Answer: That believers love one another
Reference: 2 John 5–6

Love is described as walking according to God’s commands.

What did deceivers refuse to confess?
Answer: Jesus Christ coming in the flesh
Reference: 2 John 7

The warning connects teaching about Jesus with communal boundaries.

What should readers watch?
Answer: That they not lose what had been worked for
Reference: 2 John 8–9

Remaining in Christ’s teaching is the stated safeguard.

Why did the elder not write everything?
Answer: He hoped to speak face to face
Reference: 2 John 12–13

Embodied conversation was expected to complete joy.

How to study 2 John

Read 2 John from beginning to end and summarise every mapped section in one sentence. Create columns for people, audience, problem, images, reused Scriptures and community response. Verify all six references in context, record repeated terms and explain how the ending answers the opening. Compare translations when a note changes a key word, but do not build a conclusion on one isolated variant. This process joins recall, literary structure, historical setting, ethics and application without turning a debated reading into an undisputed fact.

Context and interpretation note

Whether the chosen lady is an individual or a church is debated. The hospitality warning concerns active support for teachers who undermine the letter’s confession; it should not become blanket hostility to strangers, honest questioners or other denominations. Truth and love remain inseparable, and boundaries require careful evidence rather than suspicion.
