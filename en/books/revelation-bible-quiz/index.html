What is Revelation about?

Revelation is an apocalypse, prophecy and circular letter to seven assemblies in Roman Asia. Through visions, songs and symbolic conflict it unveils God’s throne, the slain yet victorious Lamb, resistance to beastly power and Babylon’s economy, judgment on violence and a renewed creation where God dwells with humanity.

This guide treats Revelation as a complete, situated work rather than a bag of isolated sayings. The six questions sample different units and the map locates each answer. Watch repetitions, contrasts, images, connective words and audience shifts. Check the whole paragraph whenever an answer depends on surrounding reasoning, and distinguish visionary description, scriptural quotation, argument and exhortation.

A four-part reading map

  1. 1–3 — risen Christ and seven messages: inaugural vision, local praise, correction, promise and call to hear
  2. 4–11 — throne, Lamb and opening scroll: worship, seals, sealed people, trumpets, scroll and two witnesses
  3. 12–20 — dragon, beasts and Babylon: symbolic war, endurance, bowls, empire’s fall, victory and judgment
  4. 21–22 — new creation and final invitation: holy city, divine presence, river, tree, worship and coming

Sample questions and answers

Read the cited unit before revealing the answer and explain how it functions within the work.

To which seven churches was the book addressed?
Answer: Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamum, Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia and Laodicea
Reference: Revelation 1:4–11

The visions are a circular letter to real communities.

Who was worthy to open the sealed scroll?
Answer: The Lamb who was slain
Reference: Revelation 5:1–10

Lion imagery is reinterpreted through the standing slain Lamb.

Who were sealed in chapter 7?
Answer: 144,000 from Israel’s tribes, followed by an uncountable multitude
Reference: Revelation 7:1–17

The relation between the two visions is interpreted differently.

What number is associated with the beast?
Answer: 666
Reference: Revelation 13:16–18

The text invites wisdom in calculating a human number.

What city symbolically falls in chapter 18?
Answer: Babylon the great
Reference: Revelation 18:1–24

Kings and merchants mourn an exploitative imperial economy.

What descends from heaven in the final vision?
Answer: New Jerusalem
Reference: Revelation 21:1–4

God dwells with humanity and wipes away tears.

How to study Revelation

Read Revelation from beginning to end and summarise every mapped section in one sentence. Create columns for people, audience, problem, images, reused Scriptures and community response. Verify all six references in context, record repeated terms and explain how the ending answers the opening. Compare translations when a note changes a key word, but do not build a conclusion on one isolated variant. This process joins recall, literary structure, historical setting, ethics and application without turning a debated reading into an undisputed fact.

Context and interpretation note

Revelation’s symbols have produced preterist, historicist, futurist and idealist readings, often in combination. Numbers and monsters should not be mapped confidently onto current people without evidence. Violent imagery exposes and judges empire; it must not authorise human violence, conspiracy or antisemitism. Read the visions from the Lamb’s self-giving victory toward renewed creation.
