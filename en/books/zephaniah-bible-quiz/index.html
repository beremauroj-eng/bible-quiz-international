What is Zephaniah about?

Zephaniah announces an approaching day of the Lord during the reign of Josiah. The prophecy confronts idolatry, complacency and official corruption in Judah, then widens judgment to surrounding nations. Its final movement calls humble seekers together, exposes rebellious Jerusalem and envisions a purified people over whom God rejoices.

A four-part reading map

  1. Zephaniah 1:1–13 — sweeping judgment: creation-wide language narrows to Judah, syncretistic worship, royal officials, violent commerce and complacent households.
  2. Zephaniah 1:14–2:3 — the great day and a call: trumpet, darkness and distress lead to an urgent invitation for the humble to seek righteousness.
  3. Zephaniah 2:4–15 — surrounding nations: Philistine cities, Moab, Ammon, Cush and Assyria face judgment, ending with desolate Nineveh.
  4. Zephaniah 3 — Jerusalem and restoration: corrupt institutions are charged, peoples receive purified speech, a humble remnant rests safely and Zion rejoices.

Sample questions and answers

Trace how the “day” moves from Judah to nations and back to a restored community; the prophecy includes both universal-sounding judgment and specific historical targets.

During whose reign did Zephaniah receive the word recorded in the book?
Answer: King Josiah son of Amon
Reference: Zephaniah 1:1

The heading also provides an unusually long genealogy for the prophet.

How does God say Jerusalem will be searched?
Answer: With lamps
Reference: Zephaniah 1:12–13

The search targets complacent people who assume God will do neither good nor harm.

What are the humble of the land told to seek?
Answer: The Lord, righteousness and humility
Reference: Zephaniah 2:1–3

The appeal holds out the possibility of shelter on the day of anger.

Which proud Assyrian city is pictured becoming a desolate resting place for animals?
Answer: Nineveh
Reference: Zephaniah 2:13–15

The city that considered itself unmatched becomes an object of astonishment.

How is the restored remnant described in relation to speech and safety?
Answer: They do no wrong or speak lies and rest without anyone making them afraid
Reference: Zephaniah 3:11–13

The humble remnant seeks refuge and is pictured feeding and lying down securely.

How does the Lord respond to the restored people in the closing song?
Answer: He rejoices over them with gladness and singing
Reference: Zephaniah 3:14–17

The passage also describes God as present, saving and quieting them with love.

How to study Zephaniah

Create a target map for Judah, Jerusalem’s groups and every nation named. Mark the repeated phrases “day of the Lord,” “in that day,” “seek” and “remnant.” Compare the opening reversal-of-creation language with the purified speech and gathering at the end. Place Zephaniah beside 2 Kings 22–23 to consider Josiah’s reforms, while noting that the text does not specify exactly where every message falls within that reign.

Context and interpretation note

The prophecy’s date within Josiah’s reign, its relationship to reforms and the scope of “all the earth” are debated. Day-of-the-Lord imagery can address near historical judgment while also feeding later eschatological readings. Descriptions of national judgment must not be turned into hostility toward modern peoples. The quiz tests named rulers, places, actions and promises while avoiding a single end-times timetable or claims beyond the cited text.
