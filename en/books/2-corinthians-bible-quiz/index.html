What is 2 Corinthians about?

Second Corinthians reveals a strained relationship between Paul and the Corinthian believers. Consolation, travel changes, a painful visit, a severe letter, reconciliation, the Jerusalem collection and rival teachers shape its movement. Paul defends a ministry whose credibility appears through suffering, integrity and divine power in weakness.

This guide treats 2 Corinthians as a situated argument rather than a collection of slogans. The six questions sample distinct units, while the map locates every answer. Watch repetitions, contrasts, connective words and shifts of audience. Verify an answer across more than one verse whenever its wording depends on the reasoning before or after it.

A four-part reading map

  1. 1–2 — changed plans and restored relationship: comfort, integrity, sorrow and forgiveness
  2. 3–7 — ministry of reconciliation: new covenant, fragile vessels, new creation and appeal
  3. 8–9 — generous participation: Macedonian example, accountable administration and cheerful giving
  4. 10–13 — contested apostleship: rival claims, reluctant boasting, visions, weakness and final warning

Sample questions and answers

Use each reference as an entry point and read the whole unit before selecting an answer.

What treasure is carried in jars of clay?
Answer: The light or knowledge of God’s glory in Christ
Reference: 2 Corinthians 4:6–12

Fragile messengers display power that belongs to God.

What happens to anyone in Christ?
Answer: That person is a new creation
Reference: 2 Corinthians 5:16–21

Reconciliation with God creates a reconciling vocation.

What kind of sorrow produces repentance?
Answer: Godly sorrow
Reference: 2 Corinthians 7:8–11

Paul distinguishes life-giving grief from destructive grief.

Which churches gave beyond their ability?
Answer: The Macedonian churches
Reference: 2 Corinthians 8:1–5

Their generosity supports the appeal to complete the collection.

What kind of giver does God love?
Answer: A cheerful giver
Reference: 2 Corinthians 9:6–9

Giving should be decided freely, not under compulsion.

What was sufficient for Paul in weakness?
Answer: God’s grace
Reference: 2 Corinthians 12:7–10

Power reaches its goal in weakness, reversing ordinary boasting.

How to study 2 Corinthians

Read 2 Corinthians as a complete letter before isolating memorable lines. Summarise each of the four mapped sections and note who speaks, which problem is answered and what practical consequence follows. Trace connections among changed plans and restored relationship, ministry of reconciliation, generous participation, contested apostleship. For every question, verify the full paragraph, compare transitions and distinguish assertion, quotation, metaphor and exhortation. Record repeated terms and named coworkers, then explain how the closing material returns to concerns introduced near the opening. This method keeps recall, argument, social setting and application in conversation.

Context and interpretation note

Readers debate whether the letter combines several pieces of correspondence and how to reconstruct Paul’s visits. These questions should not erase the final literary sequence. Appeals for money require attention to voluntary giving and transparent administration; Paul’s language about weakness must never excuse coercive or abusive leadership.
