What is Joshua about?

Joshua describes Israel’s entry into the land after Moses’ death, campaigns led by Joshua, the distribution of territories and a final covenant gathering. The book connects leadership transition with repeated calls to follow the instruction previously given through Moses.

A four-part reading map

  1. Joshua 1–5 — entry: Joshua’s commission, the spies and Rahab, crossing the Jordan, memorial stones and preparation at Gilgal.
  2. Joshua 6–12 — campaigns: Jericho, Achan and Ai, the Gibeonites, southern and northern battles, and a summary of defeated kings.
  3. Joshua 13–21 — allotment: tribal territories, Caleb’s inheritance, cities of refuge and Levitical cities.
  4. Joshua 22–24 — unity and covenant: the eastern tribes’ altar, Joshua’s final speeches and the assembly at Shechem.

Sample questions and answers

Answer from memory, then read the cited scene to place each detail within the book’s movement from entry to covenant renewal.

Who succeeded Moses as leader at the beginning of the book?
Answer: Joshua son of Nun
Reference: Joshua 1:1–2

The opening command tells Joshua to arise and lead the people across the Jordan.

Who hid the two Israelite spies in Jericho?
Answer: Rahab
Reference: Joshua 2:1–6

Rahab hid the spies under stalks of flax on her roof and later helped them escape.

How many stones were taken from the Jordan as a memorial?
Answer: Twelve
Reference: Joshua 4:1–9

One man from each tribe carried a stone so future generations could ask what the memorial meant.

Whose taking of devoted items led to Israel’s defeat at Ai?
Answer: Achan’s
Reference: Joshua 7:1–26

The narrative connects the hidden items with Israel’s first failed attack on Ai.

During which battle did Joshua speak of the sun standing still?
Answer: The battle associated with Gibeon
Reference: Joshua 10:6–14

Joshua spoke as Israel fought the Amorite kings after responding to Gibeon’s appeal.

What choice did Joshua place before the people at Shechem?
Answer: Whom they would serve
Reference: Joshua 24:14–24

Joshua contrasts other gods with serving the Lord and declares the choice of his household.

How to study Joshua

Track movement on a simple map: east of the Jordan, the crossing, central, southern and northern campaigns, and tribal allotments. Mark every memorial, boundary and covenant gathering. Compare Joshua’s opening commission in chapter 1 with his closing speeches in chapters 23–24 to see how leadership, courage and obedience frame the book.

Context and interpretation note

Place names and territorial boundaries differ in spelling across translations, and modern identifications are not equally certain. The campaign narratives also raise serious ethical and theological questions that communities interpret differently. This quiz tests people, sequence, speeches and stated outcomes in Joshua; it does not turn disputed modern applications or archaeological conclusions into simple factual answers.
