What is 2 Samuel about?

2 Samuel centres on David’s reign. It moves from kingship over Judah to rule over all Israel, establishment of Jerusalem, covenant promise, military success, grave wrongdoing and turmoil within David’s household and kingdom.

A four-part reading map

  1. 2 Samuel 1–5 — David becomes king: lament for Saul and Jonathan, rule in Hebron, civil conflict and capture of Jerusalem.
  2. 2 Samuel 6–10 — ark and promise: the ark comes to Jerusalem, Nathan’s oracle and victories around Israel.
  3. 2 Samuel 11–20 — sin and family crisis: Bathsheba and Uriah, Nathan’s confrontation, Amnon, Absalom and Sheba’s revolt.
  4. 2 Samuel 21–24 — closing collection: famine, warrior traditions, songs, last words, mighty men and the census.

Sample questions and answers

Follow the change from national consolidation to household conflict and its public consequences.

Where was David first anointed king over Judah?
Answer: Hebron
Reference: 2 Samuel 2:1–4

David settled in Hebron, where the people of Judah anointed him king.

Which stronghold did David capture and call the city of David?
Answer: The stronghold of Zion in Jerusalem
Reference: 2 Samuel 5:6–10

After taking the stronghold, David established his base there.

Who died after touching the ark during its first journey toward Jerusalem?
Answer: Uzzah
Reference: 2 Samuel 6:1–8

Uzzah reached toward the ark when the oxen stumbled.

Which prophet delivered the promise concerning David’s house and kingdom?
Answer: Nathan
Reference: 2 Samuel 7:1–17

Nathan first approved David’s plan, then returned with a different message received at night.

Whose husband did David arrange to have placed in the fiercest fighting?
Answer: Bathsheba’s husband, Uriah
Reference: 2 Samuel 11:14–17

David sent instructions through Uriah that led to Uriah’s death in battle.

Which son of David led a rebellion and was killed after his hair became caught in a tree?
Answer: Absalom
Reference: 2 Samuel 18:6–15

Absalom was left hanging while the battle continued, despite David’s instruction to deal gently with him.

How to study 2 Samuel

Divide David’s public victories from the crises within his household, then trace where the two spheres overlap. Record each use of messenger reports, laments and prophetic confrontation. Compare Nathan’s promise in chapter 7 with Nathan’s rebuke in chapter 12 to see how promise and accountability coexist in the narrative.

Translation and interpretation note

Accounts also found in Chronicles may select or arrange material differently. Names and numbers can vary across manuscripts and translations, while the Absalom episode is rendered with slightly different descriptions of how he was caught. Questions cite 2 Samuel directly and do not treat every action by David or his followers as approved merely because it is reported.
