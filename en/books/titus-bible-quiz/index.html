What is Titus about?

Titus directs a coworker organising communities in Crete amid disruptive teaching. It connects leadership character with sound instruction, addresses different household groups, grounds transformed conduct in God’s saving grace and mercy, and repeatedly insists that good works make teaching socially credible.

This guide treats Titus as a situated argument rather than a collection of slogans. The six questions sample distinct units, while the map locates every answer. Watch repetitions, contrasts, connective words and shifts of audience. Verify an answer across more than one verse whenever its wording depends on the reasoning before or after it.

A four-part reading map

  1. 1 — order and correction: appointing elders, character, holding trustworthy teaching and silencing disruption
  2. 2 — conduct fitting sound teaching: older and younger people, servants, grace and eager good works
  3. 3:1–8 — public life and mercy: authorities, gentleness, former condition, rebirth and useful action
  4. 3:9–15 — avoiding division and mission logistics: controversies, warnings, travel plans and practical help

Sample questions and answers

Use each reference as an entry point and read the whole unit before selecting an answer.

Where was Titus left to appoint elders?
Answer: Crete
Reference: Titus 1:5–9

Leadership selection should repair unfinished community order.

What must an overseer hold firmly?
Answer: The trustworthy message as taught
Reference: Titus 1:7–9

Sound instruction and responsible correction require character.

What has appeared bringing salvation?
Answer: The grace of God
Reference: Titus 2:11–14

Grace trains people toward self-controlled and just living.

How should believers speak of no one?
Answer: Maliciously
Reference: Titus 3:1–2

Gentleness accompanies responsible public conduct.

According to what did God save people?
Answer: Mercy, not righteous works they had done
Reference: Titus 3:3–7

Renewal by the Spirit grounds hope and good works.

What should be avoided?
Answer: Foolish controversies, genealogies and quarrels about law
Reference: Titus 3:9–11

Divisive argument is contrasted with useful action.

How to study Titus

Read Titus as a complete letter before isolating memorable lines. Summarise each of the four mapped sections and note who speaks, which problem is answered and what practical consequence follows. Trace connections among order and correction, conduct fitting sound teaching, public life and mercy, avoiding division and mission logistics. For every question, verify the full paragraph, compare transitions and distinguish assertion, quotation, metaphor and exhortation. Record repeated terms and named coworkers, then explain how the closing material returns to concerns introduced near the opening. This method keeps recall, argument, social setting and application in conversation.

Context and interpretation note

Authorship and the reconstruction of Cretan opponents are debated. The letter’s quotation about Cretans is polemical and cannot license ethnic stereotyping. Household and slavery instructions reflect ancient structures and do not validate domination. Correcting harmful teaching must retain the letter’s demands for gentleness and visible good.
