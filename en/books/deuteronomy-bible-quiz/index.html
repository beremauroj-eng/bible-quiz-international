What is Deuteronomy about?

Deuteronomy presents Moses’ final addresses as Israel prepares to cross the Jordan. The book reviews the wilderness past, restates covenant commands, explains life in the land, sets out blessings and consequences, and closes with succession and the death of Moses.

A five-part reading map

  1. Deuteronomy 1–4 — remembering the journey: review from Horeb to the edge of the land and a call to heed the teaching.
  2. Deuteronomy 5–11 — covenant foundations: Ten Commandments, the Shema, remembrance and loyalty.
  3. Deuteronomy 12–26 — community instruction: worship, leadership, justice, festivals, warfare, family and economic life.
  4. Deuteronomy 27–30 — covenant renewal: ceremony, blessings, consequences, restoration and the choice of life.
  5. Deuteronomy 31–34 — transition: Joshua’s commissioning, the song and blessing of Moses, view from Nebo and Moses’ death.

Sample questions and answers

Use these questions to connect memorable passages with their place in Moses’ wider speeches.

Where did Moses begin explaining the law before Israel entered the land?
Answer: Beyond the Jordan, in the land of Moab
Reference: Deuteronomy 1:1–5

The opening locates the speeches east of the Jordan before the crossing.

In which chapter are the Ten Commandments restated?
Answer: Deuteronomy 5
Reference: Deuteronomy 5:1–22

Moses recalls the covenant made at Horeb and repeats the commandments to the assembled people.

What declaration begins the passage commonly called the Shema?
Answer: “Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God, the Lord is one”
Reference: Deuteronomy 6:4–9

The passage continues with the command to love the Lord and teach these words diligently.

What was Israel’s future king instructed to write for himself?
Answer: A copy of the law
Reference: Deuteronomy 17:18–20

The king was to read it throughout his life so that he would learn to follow it and not exalt himself.

What choice did Moses set before the people in Deuteronomy 30?
Answer: Life and death, blessing and curse
Reference: Deuteronomy 30:15–20

Moses urges the people to choose life by loving, obeying and holding fast to the Lord.

Whom did Moses publicly commission as his successor?
Answer: Joshua
Reference: Deuteronomy 31:7–8

Moses tells Joshua to be strong and courageous before all Israel.

How to study Deuteronomy

Mark when Moses reviews the past, gives a command, states a reason or describes a future consequence. Compare the wording of the Ten Commandments in Deuteronomy 5 with Exodus 20, noting both continuity and differences in emphasis. Trace repeated verbs such as hear, remember, teach, love and choose; they provide a stronger memory framework than isolated legal details.

Translation and interpretation note

The title “Deuteronomy” comes through Greek and is often explained as “second law,” but the book functions as covenant instruction and renewal rather than a disconnected replacement law. Translations differ in Deuteronomy 6:4 and other compact expressions. Quiz answers therefore identify the passage and accept recognisable translation variants instead of enforcing a single edition’s wording.
