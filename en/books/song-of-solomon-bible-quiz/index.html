What is Song of Solomon about?

Song of Solomon, also called Song of Songs, is a collection of love poetry shaped as exchanges among lovers and a supporting chorus. Desire, admiration, absence, search, reunion, gardens, vineyards and springtime imagery recur across its eight chapters. The poems celebrate attraction and committed love while leaving speaker boundaries and an exact continuous plot partly open.

A five-part reading map

  1. Song 1:1–2:7 — attraction and delight: opening longing, mutual praise, imagined meeting places and the first refrain about awakening love.
  2. Song 2:8–3:5 — arrival and search: the beloved approaches like a gazelle, spring is announced, and a night search ends in reunion.
  3. Song 3:6–5:1 — procession and garden: Solomon’s procession, extended praise of the woman and garden imagery culminating in invitation.
  4. Song 5:2–8:4 — absence and renewed praise: a missed meeting, city search, descriptions of both lovers and another refrain.
  5. Song 8:5–14 — strength and maturity: love as strong as death, family memories, vineyard ownership and a final call to come away.

Sample questions and answers

Check the speaker and addressee supplied by the text or translation before assigning a line; many modern speaker labels are editorial aids rather than part of the Hebrew wording.

What expression of affection is requested in the poem’s opening lines?
Answer: Kisses from the beloved’s mouth
Reference: Song of Solomon 1:2–4

The speaker says the beloved’s love is better than wine and praises the fragrance of his name.

To what royal image is the woman compared in Song 1?
Answer: A mare among Pharaoh’s chariots
Reference: Song of Solomon 1:9–11

The comparison introduces praise adorned with jewellery imagery.

What animals describe the beloved moving across the mountains?
Answer: A gazelle or a young stag
Reference: Song of Solomon 2:8–9

The energetic image accompanies the arrival of spring and an invitation to rise and come away.

What creatures are to be caught because they ruin the vineyards?
Answer: The little foxes
Reference: Song of Solomon 2:15

The vineyard image appears while the vines are in bloom.

What repeated warning is addressed to the daughters of Jerusalem?
Answer: Do not arouse or awaken love until it desires
Reference: Song of Solomon 2:7; 3:5; 8:4

The refrain marks important transitions across the collection.

What is said to be as strong as death near the book’s conclusion?
Answer: Love
Reference: Song of Solomon 8:6–7

The passage adds that many waters cannot quench love and rivers cannot sweep it away.

How to study Song of Solomon

Mark changes in pronouns, audience and place, then compare how several translations assign speakers. Build an imagery list for gardens, vineyards, animals, fragrances, precious materials and seasons. Track the repeated daughters-of-Jerusalem refrain and the movement between longing, search, presence and separation. Read descriptions as poetry rather than anatomical diagrams; their comparisons communicate value, vitality, exclusivity and delight through the visual world of the poem.

Context and interpretation note

Jewish and Christian traditions have read the Song as human love poetry, allegory of divine covenant love, or both, and interpreters disagree about plot, number of characters and Solomon’s role. The Hebrew text does not include most speaker labels printed in translations. Its sensual adult imagery should be handled with age-appropriate care and without turning poetic metaphors into stereotypes or relationship rules. Quiz answers remain with explicit images, refrains and actions in cited verses.
