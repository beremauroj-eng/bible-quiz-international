What is Malachi about?

Malachi is structured as a series of disputes between God and the post-exilic community. Questions and objections expose cynical worship, defective offerings, failed priestly instruction, marital unfaithfulness and doubts about justice. The book responds with covenant accountability, a coming messenger, purification, faithful giving, a scroll of remembrance and the return of Elijah.

A six-dispute reading map

  1. Malachi 1:2–5 — disputed love: the people question divine love and receive a comparison involving Jacob and Esau.
  2. Malachi 1:6–2:9 — polluted worship: priests offer blemished animals and fail the covenantal teaching role associated with Levi.
  3. Malachi 2:10–16 — covenant relationships: communal betrayal and unfaithfulness in marriage are confronted.
  4. Malachi 2:17–3:5 — disputed justice: a messenger prepares the way and the Lord comes to purify and judge exploitation.
  5. Malachi 3:6–12 — return and giving: withheld tithes are named and restored covenant participation is invited.
  6. Malachi 3:13–4:6 — remembrance and day: faithful people speak, a book records them, the day distinguishes outcomes and Elijah is promised.

Sample questions and answers

Read each objection and response together; the community’s quoted question often reveals the attitude that the following answer addresses.

What defective offerings were priests bringing to the altar?
Answer: Blind, lame and sick animals
Reference: Malachi 1:6–14

The dispute asks whether such gifts would be acceptable to a human governor.

What roles does Malachi associate with the covenant of Levi?
Answer: Reverence, truthful instruction and turning people from wrongdoing
Reference: Malachi 2:4–9

The priest’s lips should preserve knowledge, but the addressed priests have caused many to stumble.

Who is promised before the Lord’s coming to the temple?
Answer: A messenger who prepares the way
Reference: Malachi 3:1–5

The passage then uses refiner’s fire and fuller’s soap to picture purification.

How does the book say the community was robbing God?
Answer: By withholding tithes and contributions
Reference: Malachi 3:6–12

The people are told to bring the full tithe to the storehouse and test the promised provision.

What was written for those who feared the Lord and spoke together?
Answer: A book or scroll of remembrance
Reference: Malachi 3:13–18

The image distinguishes those treasured by God from the arrogant claim that service is useless.

Which prophet is promised before the great day of the Lord?
Answer: Elijah
Reference: Malachi 4:4–6

The closing promise speaks of turning hearts across generations.

How to study Malachi

Format each dispute as claim, objection, evidence and response. Track covenant language across worship, priestly teaching, marriage and community economics. Compare the messenger passages with later Gospel citations only after establishing their position within Malachi. Note how the final remembrance scene answers the earlier complaint that evildoers prosper. Read the closing references to Moses and Elijah as a deliberate bridge joining instruction and prophecy.

Context and interpretation note

Malachi’s marriage and divorce language is textually difficult and must not be used to trap someone in abuse or erase legal and pastoral complexity. The tithe dispute concerns Israel’s temple system and does not establish a simplistic guarantee that a modern donation produces wealth. Messenger and Elijah expectations receive different Jewish and Christian interpretations. The quiz tests explicit disputes, images and named practices while avoiding coercive fundraising or unsafe relationship advice.
