What is 1 Chronicles about?

1 Chronicles retells Israel’s story from Adam to the end of David’s reign. Its opening genealogies connect the restored community with earlier generations, while the narrative gives sustained attention to David, Jerusalem, the ark, organised worship and preparations for the temple that Solomon will build.

A four-part reading map

  1. 1 Chronicles 1–9 — generations and identity: family lines from Adam, the tribes of Israel, priestly and Levitical families, and inhabitants of Jerusalem after exile.
  2. 1 Chronicles 10–16 — David, Jerusalem and the ark: Saul’s death, David’s supporters, capture of Jerusalem, two attempts to move the ark and worship before it.
  3. 1 Chronicles 17–21 — promise, victories and failure: God’s covenant promise to David, military accounts, the census and the threshing floor of Ornan.
  4. 1 Chronicles 22–29 — preparation and succession: materials, personnel, worship divisions, David’s final assembly and Solomon’s accession.

Sample questions and answers

Use the references to distinguish material shared with Samuel and Kings from the emphases Chronicles develops around worship, preparation and continuity.

With whom does the first genealogy in 1 Chronicles begin?
Answer: Adam
Reference: 1 Chronicles 1:1–4

The opening names move from Adam through Seth and Enosh toward Noah and his sons.

Which city did David capture and establish as his capital?
Answer: Jerusalem, also called the stronghold of Zion or the City of David
Reference: 1 Chronicles 11:4–9

David took the stronghold, lived there and built up the surrounding city.

What happened to Uzzah when he reached out to steady the ark?
Answer: He died after God struck him
Reference: 1 Chronicles 13:9–12

The failed first transport leads David to reconsider how the ark should be carried.

Who did God say would build the house associated with David’s promised dynasty?
Answer: David’s son
Reference: 1 Chronicles 17:7–14

The promise connects David’s descendant, the temple and an enduring royal house.

Whose threshing floor did David purchase after the census and plague?
Answer: Ornan the Jebusite’s
Reference: 1 Chronicles 21:18–28

David bought the site, built an altar and offered sacrifices there.

Whom did the assembly acknowledge as king after David’s final preparations?
Answer: Solomon
Reference: 1 Chronicles 29:20–25

Solomon was publicly installed and sat on the royal throne as David’s successor.

How to study 1 Chronicles

Do not skip the genealogies as meaningless lists. Mark where lines narrow toward Israel, Judah, Levi, Benjamin and the Jerusalem community, then note how those groups reappear in the narrative. Compare parallel episodes in Samuel without assuming that different selection or arrangement is contradiction; ask what each account chooses to emphasise. Track every reference to Levites, musicians, gatekeepers, offerings and temple resources.

Context and translation note

Ancient genealogies can be selective, may use “son” for later descendants and sometimes preserve names with spelling variations. Numbers and personal names also differ across manuscripts and translations. The quiz therefore tests relationships and events explicitly stated in 1 Chronicles and cites the passage used; it does not force uncertain family links, manuscript variants or modern chronological reconstructions into one unqualified answer.
