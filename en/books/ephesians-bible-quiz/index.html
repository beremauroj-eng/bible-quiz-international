What is Ephesians about?

Ephesians presents a panoramic vision of God’s purpose to unite all things in Christ. It moves from blessing and grace to reconciliation between Jews and Gentiles, the church’s vocation, a transformed common life, household relationships and resistance to spiritual evil. Prayer frames both theology and practice.

This guide treats Ephesians as a situated argument rather than a collection of slogans. The six questions sample distinct units, while the map locates every answer. Watch repetitions, contrasts, connective words and shifts of audience. Verify an answer across more than one verse whenever its wording depends on the reasoning before or after it.

A four-part reading map

  1. 1 — blessing and prayer: adoption, redemption, inheritance, sealing and enlightened hope
  2. 2 — grace and peace: life from death, gift rather than boasting, one new humanity and household
  3. 3 — mystery and vocation: Gentile co-heirs, Paul’s ministry and prayer for rooted love
  4. 4–6 — worthy life: unity, gifts, renewed conduct, Spirit-filled households and God’s armour

Sample questions and answers

Use each reference as an entry point and read the whole unit before selecting an answer.

By what are people saved through faith?
Answer: Grace
Reference: Ephesians 2:1–10

Salvation is God’s gift and leads into prepared good works.

What did Christ make from Jews and Gentiles?
Answer: One new humanity
Reference: Ephesians 2:11–22

The cross breaks hostility and creates shared access.

What should believers preserve?
Answer: The unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace
Reference: Ephesians 4:1–6

One body, Spirit, hope, Lord, faith, baptism and God support the appeal.

What should replace falsehood?
Answer: Speaking truth with neighbours
Reference: Ephesians 4:25–32

Renewed speech belongs with controlled anger, work and forgiveness.

With what should believers be filled?
Answer: The Spirit
Reference: Ephesians 5:15–21

Singing, gratitude and mutual submission describe the result.

What is called the sword of the Spirit?
Answer: The word of God
Reference: Ephesians 6:10–18

The armour imagery supports prayerful resistance rather than violence.

How to study Ephesians

Read Ephesians as a complete letter before isolating memorable lines. Summarise each of the four mapped sections and note who speaks, which problem is answered and what practical consequence follows. Trace connections among blessing and prayer, grace and peace, mystery and vocation, worthy life. For every question, verify the full paragraph, compare transitions and distinguish assertion, quotation, metaphor and exhortation. Record repeated terms and named coworkers, then explain how the closing material returns to concerns introduced near the opening. This method keeps recall, argument, social setting and application in conversation.

Context and interpretation note

Authorship and the original destination are debated because early manuscripts and style invite different conclusions. Household instructions arose within an unequal ancient system; they must not sanction domination or abuse. The armour is a metaphor for truthful, peaceful, prayerful endurance, not a warrant for violence against people.
