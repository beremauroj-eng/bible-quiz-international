What is Jeremiah about?

Jeremiah records prophetic words and narratives from the final decades of Judah through Jerusalem’s destruction and its aftermath. Called while young, Jeremiah confronts idolatry, injustice, false confidence in the temple and resistance to Babylon. Symbolic actions, public disputes, personal laments and messages to exiles accompany promises of restoration and a new covenant.

A five-part reading map

  1. Jeremiah 1–25 — call and sustained warning: Jeremiah’s commission, covenant charges, temple sermon, drought, confessions and the approaching Babylonian judgment.
  2. Jeremiah 26–29 — conflict and exile: trial after the temple sermon, prophetic disputes and the letter telling exiles to build, plant and seek the city’s welfare.
  3. Jeremiah 30–33 — Book of Consolation: restoration, Rachel’s weeping, new covenant and Jeremiah’s purchase of a field during siege.
  4. Jeremiah 34–45 — siege, fall and aftermath: broken slave covenant, burned scroll, imprisonment, Jerusalem’s capture, Gedaliah’s murder and flight to Egypt.
  5. Jeremiah 46–52 — nations and historical appendix: oracles against surrounding powers, extended judgment on Babylon and a concluding account of the fall.

Sample questions and answers

Because the book is not arranged as a simple chronology, note the named king and location before placing each speech or symbolic action on a timeline.

What did God tell Jeremiah before describing his appointment as a prophet?
Answer: God knew and consecrated him before he was formed and born
Reference: Jeremiah 1:4–10

Jeremiah objects that he is too young, but God commissions him to speak the words given.

What did Jeremiah see in his first recorded vision?
Answer: A branch of an almond tree
Reference: Jeremiah 1:11–12

The vision uses a Hebrew wordplay connecting the almond branch with God watching over the word.

Where was Jeremiah sent to observe clay being reshaped as a prophetic lesson?
Answer: The potter’s house
Reference: Jeremiah 18:1–12

The potter reworked a spoiled vessel, illustrating divine authority and responsive judgment.

Which king cut and burned Jeremiah’s scroll as it was read?
Answer: Jehoiakim
Reference: Jeremiah 36:1–32

The king burned sections in the fire, after which Jeremiah dictated another scroll with additional words.

What property did Jeremiah purchase while Jerusalem was under siege?
Answer: A field at Anathoth
Reference: Jeremiah 32:1–15

The sealed deed served as a sign that houses, fields and vineyards would again be bought in the land.

Where does the new-covenant promise say God’s instruction will be written?
Answer: On the people’s hearts
Reference: Jeremiah 31:31–34

The promise also speaks of knowing God and forgiveness of wrongdoing.

How to study Jeremiah

Create two parallel tools: a king-by-king timeline from Josiah to Zedekiah and a theme index for covenant, temple, false prophecy, Babylon, exile and restoration. Place each prose narrative and poem where the text provides a date, but accept that literary arrangement often groups material by theme. Track Jeremiah’s symbolic actions and personal confessions separately. Compare chapter 52 with 2 Kings 24–25 to identify the appendix’s historical function.

Context and interpretation note

The Hebrew and Greek forms of Jeremiah differ substantially in length and order, and modern translations follow different textual decisions. Dates, repeated passages and the relationship between Jeremiah and Baruch also raise compositional questions. Commands involving Babylon and the final crisis belong to a specific ancient setting and should not be transferred directly to modern politics. Quiz answers follow the cited edition’s narrative facts and flag text-critical or chronological disputes.
