What is Matthew about?

Matthew presents Jesus as Messiah, teacher and Son of Man within Israel’s story. An opening genealogy and infancy narrative lead into kingdom proclamation, healing, parables and five major teaching blocks. Conflict intensifies in Jerusalem before crucifixion, resurrection and a commission extending discipleship to all nations.

A five-part reading map

  1. Matthew 1–4 — origins and preparation: genealogy, birth, visitors, flight, John’s preaching, baptism, testing and first disciples.
  2. Matthew 5–10 — kingdom life and mission: Sermon on the Mount, healings, authority and instructions to the Twelve.
  3. Matthew 11–18 — responses and community: questions about Jesus, parables, feedings, Peter’s confession, transfiguration and teaching on communal relationships.
  4. Matthew 19–25 — journey and Jerusalem teaching: costly discipleship, entry into the city, temple controversies and sustained discourse about watchfulness.
  5. Matthew 26–28 — passion and resurrection: final meal, Gethsemane, trials, crucifixion, burial, empty tomb and final commission.

Sample questions and answers

Track how teaching and action interpret one another; Matthew often groups material thematically and repeatedly connects Jesus’ story with Israel’s Scriptures.

Which two ancestors are highlighted in Matthew’s opening description of Jesus?
Answer: David and Abraham
Reference: Matthew 1:1–17

The genealogy structures generations around Abraham, David, exile and the Messiah.

Who came from the east seeking the newborn king?
Answer: Magi or wise men
Reference: Matthew 2:1–12

They followed a star, consulted in Jerusalem and presented gifts after finding the child.

Who are called blessed first in the Beatitudes?
Answer: The poor in spirit
Reference: Matthew 5:1–12

The opening blessing says the kingdom of heaven belongs to them.

What confession did Peter make near Caesarea Philippi?
Answer: Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of the living God
Reference: Matthew 16:13–20

The confession is followed by teaching about Jesus’ suffering and the path of discipleship.

Which commandments did Jesus identify as the greatest?
Answer: Love God fully and love your neighbour as yourself
Reference: Matthew 22:34–40

Jesus says the Law and Prophets depend on these two commandments.

What mission did the risen Jesus give the disciples?
Answer: Make disciples of all nations, baptising and teaching them
Reference: Matthew 28:16–20

The commission closes with the promise of Jesus’ presence to the end of the age.

How to study Matthew

Outline the five major discourses and note the narrative sections around each. Create a fulfilment table recording the earlier text Matthew cites, its original context and the Gospel’s use. Compare repeated mountain, kingdom, righteousness and “little faith” language. When using a Synoptic parallel, first identify Matthew’s distinctive wording and placement rather than blending the accounts into one composite story.

Context and interpretation note

Matthew emerged from first-century Jewish and early Christian contexts and must not be read as permission for anti-Jewish prejudice. “Fulfilment,” kingdom language, church discipline, divorce, final judgment and the passion narrative require careful historical and pastoral interpretation. Manuscripts and translations differ in some wording. The quiz tests Matthew’s stated sequence, speakers and teachings while distinguishing them from harmonised details imported from other Gospels.
