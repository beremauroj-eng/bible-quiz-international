What is 2 Timothy about?

Second Timothy portrays an imprisoned Paul urging Timothy not to be ashamed, to preserve and transmit sound teaching, endure hardship, handle Scripture responsibly, correct opponents gently and continue ministry as Paul anticipates death. Personal names show both abandonment and durable partnership.

This guide treats 2 Timothy as a situated argument rather than a collection of slogans. The six questions sample distinct units, while the map locates every answer. Watch repetitions, contrasts, connective words and shifts of audience. Verify an answer across more than one verse whenever its wording depends on the reasoning before or after it.

A four-part reading map

  1. 1 — rekindled courage: heritage, gift, suffering, guarded deposit and loyal Onesiphorus
  2. 2 — reliable transmission: soldier, athlete, farmer, remembering Christ and gentle correction
  3. 3 — difficult times and Scripture: distorted character, persecution, learned sources and equipped service
  4. 4 — final charge and testimony: proclaiming, finishing the race, deserted hearing and coworkers

Sample questions and answers

Use each reference as an entry point and read the whole unit before selecting an answer.

What should Timothy fan into flame?
Answer: The gift of God
Reference: 2 Timothy 1:3–7

The appeal opposes fear with power, love and self-discipline.

To whom should teaching be entrusted?
Answer: Reliable people able to teach others
Reference: 2 Timothy 2:1–2

The verse sketches a multi-generation chain of transmission.

What three workers illustrate endurance?
Answer: A soldier, athlete and farmer
Reference: 2 Timothy 2:3–7

Each image highlights focus, rules or patient labour.

How should opponents be corrected?
Answer: With gentleness
Reference: 2 Timothy 2:22–26

The aim is repentance and escape from error, not humiliation.

What is God-breathed and useful?
Answer: All Scripture
Reference: 2 Timothy 3:14–17

Its uses equip God’s servant for every good work.

What had Paul finished?
Answer: The race
Reference: 2 Timothy 4:6–8

He also says he fought the good fight and kept the faith.

How to study 2 Timothy

Read 2 Timothy as a complete letter before isolating memorable lines. Summarise each of the four mapped sections and note who speaks, which problem is answered and what practical consequence follows. Trace connections among rekindled courage, reliable transmission, difficult times and Scripture, final charge and testimony. For every question, verify the full paragraph, compare transitions and distinguish assertion, quotation, metaphor and exhortation. Record repeated terms and named coworkers, then explain how the closing material returns to concerns introduced near the opening. This method keeps recall, argument, social setting and application in conversation.

Context and interpretation note

Authorship and the historical setting are debated. “All Scripture” refers first to sacred writings Timothy had learned and should be read within the paragraph’s purposes, not as a shortcut around interpretation. Military and combat metaphors concern disciplined endurance in teaching, never physical or verbal violence against opponents.
