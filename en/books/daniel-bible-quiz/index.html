What is Daniel about?

Daniel combines court narratives about Judeans serving foreign kings with symbolic visions of kingdoms, persecution and divine judgment. Stories of food, dreams, a furnace, royal pride, mysterious writing and a lions’ den model faithfulness under imperial pressure. Later chapters use apocalyptic imagery to affirm that violent powers are limited and accountable.

A five-part reading map

  1. Daniel 1 — identity in exile: Daniel and companions receive Babylonian education, request alternative food and are found exceptionally capable.
  2. Daniel 2–4 — dreams and royal power: the statue of kingdoms, the fiery furnace and Nebuchadnezzar’s tree dream and humiliation.
  3. Daniel 5–6 — two threatened lives: Belshazzar’s feast and handwriting, followed by Darius’s decree and Daniel in the lions’ den.
  4. Daniel 7–9 — beasts, sanctuary and prayer: four beasts, the Ancient of Days, ram and goat, Daniel’s confession and the seventy weeks.
  5. Daniel 10–12 — final vision: heavenly conflict, successive rulers, severe persecution, deliverance and resurrection hope.

Sample questions and answers

Distinguish the story’s named empires from symbolic figures inside visions; interpretation should begin with the explanation the chapter itself supplies.

What food arrangement did Daniel request instead of the royal provisions?
Answer: Vegetables or plant food to eat and water to drink for a test period
Reference: Daniel 1:8–16

After ten days the four Judeans appeared healthy, and the arrangement continued.

Who were thrown into the blazing furnace for refusing to worship the golden image?
Answer: Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego
Reference: Daniel 3:8–27

The king then saw a fourth figure with them, and the three emerged unharmed.

What words appeared in the handwriting at Belshazzar’s feast?
Answer: Mene, Mene, Tekel and Parsin
Reference: Daniel 5:22–28

Daniel interpreted them as numbering, weighing and division of the kingdom.

Why was Daniel placed in the lions’ den?
Answer: He continued praying to God despite the royal prohibition
Reference: Daniel 6:6–23

Daniel prayed three times daily, and the king later found him alive because the lions’ mouths were shut.

Who received everlasting dominion before the Ancient of Days in Daniel 7?
Answer: One like a son of man, representing the holy people in the interpretation
Reference: Daniel 7:9–14, 18, 27

The vision contrasts this enduring rule with beastly kingdoms that lose authority.

What future hope is announced for many who sleep in the dust?
Answer: They will awake, some to everlasting life and others to shame
Reference: Daniel 12:1–3

The wise are pictured shining while the final vision moves toward its sealed conclusion.

How to study Daniel

Chart rulers and settings in chapters 1–6, then create a separate symbol table for chapters 7–12 with columns for image, interpretation given in the text and questions left open. Notice the book’s Hebrew and Aramaic sections and repeated movement between human decrees and divine sovereignty. Compare the four-kingdom patterns across chapters without forcing every detail to map identically. Read the prayers in chapters 2 and 9 alongside the visions.

Context and interpretation note

Daniel’s date, empire identifications, seventy weeks, final ruler and fulfilment horizons are strongly disputed among interpreters. Catholic and Orthodox Bibles also include Greek additions not present in the Jewish and most Protestant arrangement. Apocalyptic numbers and creatures communicate through symbol and should not be converted casually into predictions about current headlines.
