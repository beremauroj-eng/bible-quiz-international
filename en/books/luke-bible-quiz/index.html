What is Luke about?

Luke offers an orderly narrative of Jesus’ life as the first volume of a two-part work continued in Acts. It highlights the Holy Spirit, prayer, meals, women, people at social margins, wealth and poverty, forgiveness and joy. A long journey toward Jerusalem gathers distinctive parables and teaching before passion, resurrection and promised witness.

A four-part reading map

  1. Luke 1–2 — births and songs: announcements concerning John and Jesus, Mary’s visit, poetic praise, Bethlehem, shepherds, temple witnesses and the young Jesus.
  2. Luke 3–9 — preparation and Galilean mission: John, baptism, genealogy, testing, Nazareth, healings, calls, teaching, parables and Peter’s confession.
  3. Luke 9:51–19:27 — journey to Jerusalem: mission, prayer, hospitality, contested meals and parables including the Samaritan, lost children and rich man.
  4. Luke 19:28–24:53 — Jerusalem and resurrection: entry, temple teaching, final meal, trials, crucifixion, empty tomb, Emmaus and ascension.

Sample questions and answers

Watch how Luke pairs scenes and characters, and note who is welcomed, reversed, restored or invited to respond with joy.

Which angel announced to Mary that she would give birth to Jesus?
Answer: Gabriel
Reference: Luke 1:26–38

The announcement connects Jesus with Davidic kingship and divine sonship.

Who received the angelic announcement of Jesus’ birth near Bethlehem?
Answer: Shepherds watching their flocks
Reference: Luke 2:8–20

They found Mary, Joseph and the baby and then reported what they had heard.

Who acted as neighbour to the wounded traveller in Jesus’ parable?
Answer: A Samaritan
Reference: Luke 10:25–37

The Samaritan gave practical care after a priest and Levite passed by.

What did the father do when he saw his returning younger son?
Answer: Ran to him, embraced him and celebrated
Reference: Luke 15:11–32

The parable also leaves the older brother confronting the father’s welcome.

What did Zacchaeus promise to do after welcoming Jesus?
Answer: Give half to the poor and repay fourfold anyone he had defrauded
Reference: Luke 19:1–10

Jesus declares that salvation has come to the house and describes his mission to seek the lost.

Where did two disciples recognise the risen Jesus in the breaking of bread?
Answer: Emmaus
Reference: Luke 24:13–35

Jesus had interpreted the Scriptures during the journey before their eyes were opened at the meal.

How to study Luke

Read Luke and Acts as connected volumes and track promises that continue across both. Build theme chains for Spirit, prayer, meals, possessions, reversal and joy. Compare the paired infancy scenes for John and Jesus, then map the travel section geographically and thematically. When using Synoptic parallels, preserve Luke’s distinctive placement and wording, especially in parables found only here. Observe how Scripture interpretation and table fellowship frame the Emmaus conclusion.

Context and interpretation note

Luke’s historical preface, census details, chronology and relationship to other Gospels are debated. Wealth and poverty teachings, portrayals of women, Jews, Samaritans and Roman officials require historical care and must not support prejudice or paternalism. Manuscripts vary in several verses. The quiz tests Luke’s sequence and explicit teaching while distinguishing it from Acts and from familiar details supplied by Matthew, Mark or later tradition.
