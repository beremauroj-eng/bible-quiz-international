What is 2 Chronicles about?

2 Chronicles continues with Solomon’s reign and the construction and dedication of the Jerusalem temple. After the kingdom divides, it follows the rulers of Judah rather than providing a parallel history of every northern king. Temple worship, prophetic warnings, reform, invasion, exile and the possibility of return shape the book.

A four-part reading map

  1. 2 Chronicles 1–9 — Solomon and the temple: wisdom, construction, dedication, divine response, royal achievements and the Queen of Sheba.
  2. 2 Chronicles 10–20 — division and dependence: Rehoboam, Abijah, Asa and Jehoshaphat amid conflict, alliances and prophetic instruction.
  3. 2 Chronicles 21–28 — decline and interruption: rulers from Jehoram to Ahaz, Athaliah’s seizure of power, Joash’s rescue and repeated departures from covenant worship.
  4. 2 Chronicles 29–36 — reform, judgment and return: Hezekiah and Josiah, the final rulers, Jerusalem’s fall, exile and Cyrus’s proclamation.

Sample questions and answers

Read each answer within the book’s repeated pattern: a ruler’s response to instruction affects worship, leadership and the nation’s condition.

What did Solomon ask God to give him for governing the people?
Answer: Wisdom and knowledge
Reference: 2 Chronicles 1:7–12

God granted the request and also promised wealth, possessions and honour.

What filled the temple after Solomon finished praying at its dedication?
Answer: Fire came down, and the glory of the Lord filled the temple
Reference: 2 Chronicles 7:1–3

The priests could not enter because the divine glory filled the building.

Which queen came to Jerusalem to test Solomon with difficult questions?
Answer: The Queen of Sheba
Reference: 2 Chronicles 9:1–9

She observed Solomon’s wisdom and the order of his court before praising what she had seen.

Who hid the young Joash when Athaliah attempted to destroy the royal family?
Answer: Jehoshabeath, with Jehoiada the priest
Reference: 2 Chronicles 22:10–12

Joash remained concealed in the temple for six years while Athaliah ruled.

Which king reopened and repaired the temple before celebrating Passover?
Answer: Hezekiah
Reference: 2 Chronicles 29:1–36; 30:1–27

Hezekiah began temple cleansing early in his reign and invited the people to the feast.

Which Persian ruler issued the proclamation recorded at the end of 2 Chronicles?
Answer: Cyrus king of Persia
Reference: 2 Chronicles 36:22–23

Cyrus announced that he was commissioned to build a house in Jerusalem and permitted people to go up.

How to study 2 Chronicles

Create a ruler table with columns for response to God, temple policy, prophetic encounter, military pressure and final outcome. This prevents similarly named rulers and overlapping events from blending together. Compare Kings when useful, but let Chronicles determine the immediate context of a question. Track the verbs “seek,” “humble,” “forsake” and “return,” because they reveal the narrative’s own evaluation of leadership.

Context and interpretation note

Chronicles presents a selective theological history, not a modern exhaustive chronicle. Regnal numbers, battle totals and names can vary between manuscripts or translations, and compressed accounts may omit long intervals. The quiz uses statements anchored to cited passages in 2 Chronicles. It avoids treating debated chronology, archaeology or a narrator’s theological explanation as though every interpreter reaches the same modern historical conclusion.
