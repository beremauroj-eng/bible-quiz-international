What you will learn

Sample questions and answers

Who was Israel’s first anointed king?
Answer: Saul
Reference: 1 Samuel 10:1, 20–24

Samuel anointed Saul, who was then publicly selected and presented as king over Israel.

In which testament is the book of 1 Kings found?
Answer: Old Testament
Reference: 1 Kings

1 Kings is part of the Old Testament.

Which king is named in Proverbs 1:1 and traditionally associated with many Proverbs?
Answer: Solomon
Reference: Proverbs 1:1; 10:1; 25:1

Proverbs 1:1 names Solomon, and several sections of Proverbs are explicitly associated with him.

Bible fact check: Which king was famous for asking God for wisdom?
Answer: Solomon
Reference: 1 Kings 3:5–12

Solomon asked for an understanding heart to govern well.

How to use this topic

Answer first without consulting a prompt. Then open the explanation, verify the reference and note any names, places or sequences you did not remember. Repeat the topic later and compare your accuracy and response time.

Editorial approach

Questions are written to describe biblical content and avoid presenting a disputed denominational interpretation as a universal fact.

Build a reliable topic study

Use Kings of Israel and Judah Bible Quiz as a route through several biblical books, not as a substitute for reading them. First list every person, place and event named in the questions. Read each cited paragraph with the material immediately before and after it, recording the speaker, audience, narrative sequence and any repeated image. Build a timeline when the topic crosses different periods and a location map when journeys, empires or kingdoms affect the answer.

After the first attempt, classify every item as direct recall, sequence, comparison or interpretation. Verify recall in the cited verse, but test interpretive claims against the complete literary unit. Compare two translations where wording differs, and mark questions that depend on a textual, chronological or historical debate. Explain one connection between this topic and a neighbouring book guide. For every missed answer, write a two-sentence evidence note and revisit it after two days and one week. This process turns a score into a durable study record, exposes confident guessing and makes progress measurable.

For group use, assign one reader to locate the passage, another to summarise its context and a third to challenge whether the proposed answer says more than the text. Record unresolved questions rather than forcing agreement. A facilitator can then separate factual recall from denominational interpretation and direct learners back to the cited evidence.
